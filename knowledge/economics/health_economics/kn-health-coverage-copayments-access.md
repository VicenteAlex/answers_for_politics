## Scope
Effects of central-government health reforms in Spain (2012 coverage restriction and co-payments; 2018 restoration; 2021 and 2026 co-payment exemptions) on access and health, and how far aggregate indicators can be attributed to them.

## Coverage restriction (RDL 16/2012)
The strongest evidence (Juanmartí et al. 2021, grade B) finds a rise in amenable mortality of undocumented immigrants after September 2012, with external causes as a placebo. Jiménez-Rubio and Vall Castelló (2020, B) show the mechanism: less planned care, imperfect substitution by emergencies, larger where regions enforced the rule. Exposure varied because several regions kept partial coverage until the Constitutional Court annulled their norms. No peer-reviewed evaluation of the 2018 restoration (RDL 7/2018) was found; NGO reports (grade D) document uneven early application.

## Pharmaceutical co-payments
The 2012 reform made pensioners pay 10% (capped) and raised the share for higher-income workers. Regional ITS and DiD studies find large short-run falls in dispensed volume (>10% in most regions; part may be waste or stockpiling), moderate and largely temporary adherence losses for costlier chronic drugs among pensioners, larger effects in low-income groups, and in one study more hospitalisations among schizophrenia patients. One survey-based DiD in Catalonia (Martínez-Jiménez et al. 2021, IJERPH, grade C; no card) found no significant change in self-reported medicine use, a counterpoint on the extensive margin. Removing co-payments for low-income pensioners (Valencia 2016) raised prescriptions about 6-7% at a cost larger than the subsidy; the 2021 national exemption has not been evaluated.

## Waiting lists, spending and staff
Evidence is mainly descriptive (SISLE, EU-SILC, health accounts). Waiting times depend on regional management, demand (ageing, population growth: 45.4 to 48.3 million card holders 2019-2025), staff supply and shocks (COVID-19). No credible causal study attributing national waiting-list trends to a specific central government was found.

## Open questions
- Health effects of the 2021 and 2026 co-payment exemptions.
- Effective access of undocumented immigrants after 2018 and after RD 180/2026.
- Causal effect of spending cuts and staff-replacement limits (2012-2015) on waiting times and outcomes.
