## Scope
Country-agnostic synthesis on how housing supply conditions shape the response of rents and sale prices to demand, and how the rental and sale markets interact.

## Mechanisms
- **Supply elasticity**: new construction responds to prices only where land is available and planning allows. Geography (sea, slopes) and regulation (zoning, slow land development) make supply inelastic, so demand increases (jobs, migration, household formation, tourism, foreign buyers) show up as price increases.
- **Asset pricing**: the sale price of a dwelling reflects expected future rents (or owner-occupier use value) discounted by interest rates and risk. Lower interest rates or higher expected rents raise prices; higher regulatory risk on rental income lowers investor valuations.
- **Tenure choice**: households choose between renting and buying given relative costs, credit access (down payments, guarantees) and availability. If rentals become scarce, some renters with access to credit buy, adding demand to the sale market; those without credit (young, low-income, recent migrants) cannot.
- **Competing uses**: conversion of residential units to tourist or seasonal use removes them from long-term residential supply.

## Evidence by claim
- Inelastic supply: Saiz (2010) for US metros; the Banco de España (2026) documents limited supply elasticity and a 750,000-dwelling deficit in Spain, concentrated in six provinces including Barcelona.
- Common drivers: nationally in 2024 new-contract rents (+8.8%) and sale prices (+7-8%) grew at similar rates across regulated and unregulated regions (Pinto 2026).
- Tourist rentals: Garcia-López et al. (2020) find causal effects of Airbnb on Barcelona rents and prices.

## Open debates
- Relative weight of supply constraints vs demand drivers (immigration, foreign and investor demand, interest rates) in Spain's 2022-2026 price growth; causal decompositions are lacking.
- Whether planning reform or public construction can raise supply fast enough in built-out cities such as Barcelona.

## Transfer to Spain
Barcelona city is geographically constrained (sea, Collserola) and largely built out; metropolitan supply and transport matter. The joint rise of rents and sale prices across Spain, including regions without rent caps, is consistent with common supply-demand drivers. Isolating any additional effect of rent regulation on sale prices requires comparisons between regulated and unregulated areas with credible counterfactuals.
