## Scope
Effects of immigration on the wages and employment of native workers (and earlier immigrants) in high-income countries, plus the labour-market effects of the main policy levers: enforcement/removals and regularisation. Fiscal, demographic and housing effects are in kn-immigration-fiscal-demographic-housing-effects; crime, legal status and politics in kn-immigration-crime-legal-status-enforcement.

## Mechanisms
- **Supply (competition)**: more workers with similar skills -> lower wages or fewer jobs for close substitutes, if demand and capital do not adjust.
- **Complementarity**: immigrants often fill manual/low-language tasks; natives move to communication-intensive or supervisory tasks, which can raise their wages.
- **Demand and capital**: immigrants consume, rent and start firms; capital adjusts over time. Long-run effects on the average wage tend to zero in standard models if capital adjusts.
- **Technique/absorption**: firms adopt more labour-intensive techniques (González & Ortega 2011).
- **Rigidities**: where wages cannot fall (collective agreements, minimum wage), adjustment falls on employment, often of entrants (Dustmann et al. 2017).
- **Downgrading**: immigrants work below their formal qualification, so they compete with lower-skilled natives than education data suggest (Dustmann et al. 2016).

## Evidence by claim
**claim-imm-lm-average-wage-effect-small (strong, majority).** The 2005 meta-analysis of 348 estimates finds a small negative effect on similar natives; the US National Academies (2017) conclude long-run effects are "very small". The strongest contrary evidence is the Czech-commuter natural experiment (Dustmann, Schönberg & Stuhler 2017), with sharp native employment losses, in a setting with commuters (who spend elsewhere) and rigid wages.

**claim-imm-lm-low-skill-substitutes-lose-short-run (contested, divided).** The Mariel episode is the emblematic dispute: Card (1990) finds no effect; Borjas (2017) finds a 10-30% wage fall for dropouts; Peri & Yasenov (2019) find no significant effect with synthetic controls. National skill-cell estimates (Borjas 2003: -3 to -4% per 10% supply increase) are relative effects that do not translate one-to-one into total effects. Spanish regional evidence for 2001-2006 finds no effect on unskilled workers. The crux: which comparison group, which native group, short vs long run, and how rigid wages are.

**claim-imm-lm-adjustment-through-employment-growth (moderate).** Spain absorbed a 7-point rise in the foreign-born share in five years mostly through employment growth; Miami similarly. Spain's later experience (foreign unemployment 39% in 2013 vs 25% for Spaniards; KB indicators es-unemployment-rate-foreign, es-unemployment-rate-spanish) shows that in a recession immigrants bear larger employment losses.

**claim-imm-lm-enforcement-does-not-raise-native-employment (moderate, one main study).** Secure Communities reduced employment of likely undocumented workers and also of US-born workers (East et al. 2023). Border walls had negligible wage effects for natives in a structural model (Allen et al. 2018).

**claim-imm-lm-regularisation-formalises-employment (moderate).** Spain 2005: formal employment of immigrants up, informal employment of low-skilled natives and immigrants down, natives' employment unaffected, no magnet effect detected (Elias, Monras & Vázquez-Grenno 2025).

## Open debates
- Size of short-run losses for the lowest-skilled natives (Mariel dispute; measurement error vs. real effects).
- Whether effects are larger in Europe's more rigid labour markets than in the US.
- Productivity: whether large low-skill inflows slow productivity growth by delaying automation/capital deepening (Banco de España 2024 preliminary results describe this as highly uncertain: study-gavilan-2024-cinco-piezas-inmigracion-espana).
- Long-run expectation effects of repeated regularisations (magnet effects beyond the short window studied).

## Transfer to Spain
- Spain's inflows are large relative to population (foreign-born population from 7.0 million in Jan 2020 to 10.0 million in Jan 2026, indicator es-population-foreign-born), concentrated in low-wage services, agriculture, construction and care.
- Spain has high structural unemployment and segmented contracts: adjustment may fall on employment and entry rather than wages, as in the German case.
- The unemployment gap (foreign 14.5% vs Spanish 9.3% in Q3 2026, indicators es-unemployment-rate-foreign / es-unemployment-rate-spanish) shows immigrants' weaker labour-market position, not by itself an effect on natives.
- Policy relevance: proposals to stop inflows, remove irregular residents or abolish arraigo (Vox) or restrict regularisations and use points/quotas (PP) would mainly act through these channels; the evidence does not support large native employment gains from removals.
