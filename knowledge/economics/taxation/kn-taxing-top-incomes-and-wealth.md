## Scope
How much the richest pay relative to their income and wealth, why, and what proposals to raise it (minimum taxes on billionaires, harmonised wealth taxes, capital-income reforms) are expected to do. Behavioural responses to wealth taxes are detailed in [kn-wealth-tax-behavioural-responses].

## Key distinctions
- **Statutory vs effective rates**: statutory marginal rates (e.g. top IRPF bracket) apply to taxable income; effective rates divide taxes actually paid by a broader income concept. At the very top, most economic income is unrealised gains or retained corporate profits, so effective rates on economic income can be far below statutory rates.
- **Income vs wealth denominators**: "billionaires pay 0.3% of their wealth" (wealth denominator) and "the top 1% pays 24% of its income" (income denominator) are different metrics and cannot be compared directly.
- **Personal vs total taxes**: total effective rates include corporate taxes attributed to owners; personal rates exclude them. In France, billionaires' total rate of about 27% was 1.7% personal income tax plus about 25% corporate tax (IEF 2026 summary of Bach et al. 2024).
- **Survey vs administrative data**: Spanish estimates (Fedea) impute from household surveys that under-represent the very top.

## Mechanisms
- **Non-realisation and retention**: if a founder does not sell shares and the firm retains profits, little taxable personal income arises; corporate tax is paid at firm level.
- **Holding companies**: dividends received by a holding company are largely exempt from corporate tax (Spain: 95% exempt since 2021 under the participation exemption), so profits can be reinvested without personal tax until distributed.
- **Collective investment vehicles**: SICAVs (1% corporate tax if they meet shareholder requirements) and investment funds defer personal taxation until redemption; SOCIMIs (REITs) are taxed at 0% at entity level with distribution obligations.
- **Dual income tax**: capital income is taxed on a separate, lower scale than labour income (Spain's savings scale tops at 30% from 2025), lowering average rates for capital-rich households.
- **Indirect taxes and contributions**: regressive in relation to income; employer social contributions weigh little at the top.
- **Offshore evasion**: concentrated at the top; reduced but not eliminated by CRS.

## Evidence by claim
**claim-top-effective-tax-rate-falls-at-very-top** (moderate, majority). Spain: Fedea's 2022 imputation gives 27.5% for the bottom quintile, 38.8% for percentiles 91-99 and 24.1% for the top 1% [study-lopez-laborda-2025-observatorio-reparto-impuestos]. International administrative studies summarised by the EU Tax Observatory and the IEF show the same pattern at the top 0.01%/billionaires [study-alstadsaeter-2023-global-tax-evasion-report-2024; study-gomez-antonio-2026-tributacion-grandes-patrimonios]. Crux: attributing retained earnings (taxed later, upon distribution or sale) to current income; and foreign corporate taxes (Fedea shows that even adding them, the top 1% would be at about the level of the second quintile).

Deferral is not exemption: gains are taxed when realised, unless step-up at death or exit applies (Spain taxes inheritances but regions grant large reductions).

## Open debates
- Is the top-end "regressivity" mainly timing (deferral) or permanent (step-up, inheritance reliefs, emigration before realisation)?
- Best instrument: wealth tax, minimum tax on a broad income concept including unrealised gains (presumptive income), stricter holding-company rules, higher savings-base rates, or inheritance taxation (OECD favours the latter two).
- Unilateral vs coordinated implementation: exit taxes and "collector of last resort" rules vs risk of relocation to non-participating jurisdictions.
- Valuation of unlisted equity and liquidity of founders.

## Conditions for transfer to Spain
- Spain already has a net wealth tax (IP, regional) plus a state floor (ITSGF); its yield is small: IP about EUR 1.35bn paid for 2022 and about EUR 2.15bn declared result for 2024 (AEAT), plus EUR 623M from the ITSGF in its first year (devengo 2022), against total tax revenue of about 37-38% of GDP (indicator es-tax-revenue-pct-gdp).
- Spanish top wealth is dominated by (often unlisted) corporate equity, which benefits from family-business exemptions in IP and 95% reductions in inheritance tax.
- No Spanish study yet measures total effective rates of the top 0.1%/0.01% with administrative data; the AEAT and IEF have the data (IEF 2026).
