## Scope
Annual net wealth taxes on individuals (e.g. Spain's Impuesto sobre el Patrimonio and the state "solidarity" surtax on large fortunes; Norway; Switzerland; former Danish, Swedish, French taxes; Colombia). Minimum taxes on billionaires and effective tax rates at the top are covered in [kn-taxing-top-incomes-and-wealth]. This note is country-agnostic; transfer conditions to Spain are listed at the end.

## Mechanisms
- **Reporting/avoidance margin**: taxpayers can lower assessed wealth by moving assets into exempt categories (qualifying business shares, primary residence up to a cap), by using liability caps (Spain: IRPF+IP cannot exceed 60% of the income tax base, with IP reduced up to 80%), by valuation choices for unlisted firms (book value), by taking on debt, or by hiding assets offshore.
- **Real saving margin**: a wealth tax lowers the net return (substitution effect, less saving) but also lowers wealth (income effect, more saving/work). Theory is ambiguous; Ring (2025) finds income effects dominate for ordinary wealth-tax payers.
- **Mobility margin**: changing residence (within country if taxes are regional; abroad otherwise). Moving also takes the income-tax base away, so revenue losses can exceed the wealth tax itself (Agrawal et al. 2025).
- **Capital reallocation**: with heterogeneous returns, a wealth tax hits idle/low-return wealth harder than productive wealth (Guvenen et al. 2023); critics argue it also taxes illiquid founders and can reduce entrepreneurship.
- **Administration**: valuation of non-traded assets, liquidity, information exchange (CRS) and third-party reporting determine both revenue and avoidance.

## Evidence by claim
**claim-wealth-tax-reported-wealth-elastic** (strong, broad). Every credible design finds a response of taxable wealth. Magnitudes vary by an order of magnitude: Sweden 0.09-0.27 [study-seim-2017-wealth-taxes-sweden]; Denmark clear effects, larger at the top [study-jakobsen-2020-wealth-taxation-denmark]; Switzerland +43% per 1 pp after six years [study-brulhart-2022-wealth-taxes-switzerland]; Colombia immediate and persistent drops [study-londono-velez-2025-wealth-taxation-colombia]; Catalonia -3.21% over four years per 0.1 pp average rate [study-mas-montserrat-2025-avoidance-wealth-tax-catalonia]. Scheuer and Slemrod stress the dependence on design [study-scheuer-2021-taxing-our-wealth].

**claim-wealth-tax-response-mainly-avoidance** (moderate, majority). Swedish, Swiss, Colombian and Catalan studies attribute most of the response to under-reporting, offshoring and reshuffling into exempt assets; Norway shows saving rises [study-ring-2025-wealth-taxation-saving-norway]. Counterpoint: the Danish model implies large long-run effects on accumulation at the very top, which matter most for inequality and revenue over decades [study-jakobsen-2020-wealth-taxation-denmark].

**claim-wealth-tax-mobility-within-country** (moderate, majority). In Spain, after 2011 wealthy taxpayers shifted reported residence toward Madrid (100% credit); the induced IRPF revenue loss was about six times the IP loss and harmonisation would have raised even Madrid's revenue [study-agrawal-2025-wealth-tax-mobility-spain]. Swiss cantons: 24% of the response is mobility [study-brulhart-2022-wealth-taxes-switzerland]. Top earners also moved across Spanish regions in response to IRPF rates (elasticity 0.85), though higher rates still raised revenue [study-agrawal-2019-relocation-rich-spain].

**claim-wealth-tax-international-migration-modest** (moderate, majority). Scandinavia: about 2% fewer wealthy taxpayers per 1 pp, small aggregate economic effects [study-jakobsen-2024-taxing-top-wealth-migration]. UK non-doms: modest emigration and large rises in UK tax paid [study-advani-2023-taxation-migration-super-rich]. Contradicting claims (e.g. Norway after 2022, Spanish taxpayers moving to Portugal) come from press reports cited by [study-enache-2024-high-cost-wealth-taxes] and lack causal designs.

**claim-wealth-tax-real-effects-small** (weak, divided). Credible quasi-experimental evidence is limited but points to small effects [study-ring-2025-wealth-taxation-saving-norway; study-jakobsen-2024-taxing-top-wealth-migration; study-mas-montserrat-2025-avoidance-wealth-tax-catalonia]. Theory/models disagree: return-heterogeneity models predict efficiency gains [study-guvenen-2023-use-it-or-lose-it]; the OECD judges wealth taxes more distortive than capital income taxes [study-oecd-2018-net-wealth-taxes]; advocacy models predict output losses [study-enache-2024-high-cost-wealth-taxes].

**claim-wealth-tax-revenue-low-design-dependent** (strong, broad). Repeal wave and low yields [study-oecd-2018-net-wealth-taxes; study-scheuer-2021-taxing-our-wealth; study-enache-2024-high-cost-wealth-taxes]; Spanish avoidance via exemptions and the 60% cap [study-mas-montserrat-2025-avoidance-wealth-tax-catalonia]. Supporters (e.g. the Spanish IEF) argue the cause is fixable design and lack of international cooperation [study-gomez-antonio-2026-tributacion-grandes-patrimonios].

## Open debates
- **Long-run vs short-run**: reduced-form studies capture a few years; the cumulative effect on top wealth (desired by proponents as reducing concentration) and on business investment is mostly model-based.
- **Who is studied**: most evidence covers the moderately wealthy; billionaires (whose wealth is mostly founder equity in a few firms) are rarely identified.
- **Valuation and liquidity**: taxing unlisted firm equity at market value vs book value; exemptions for family firms are the main leakage but also the main protection against forced sales.
- **Temporary vs permanent**: Colombia shows hysteresis in under-reporting after temporary taxes.
- **Migration**: cross-border elasticities may rise with large rate gaps and with special regimes abroad.

## Conditions for transfer to Spain
- Spain's IP is ceded to regions with regulatory power; several regions grant 100% credits, which recreates the within-country competition studied by Agrawal et al. (2025). The state ITSGF (2022-) acts as a floor for wealth above EUR 3M net, which reduces the incentive to move between regions for that group but not to move abroad.
- Spain exempts qualifying family-business shares and applies a 60% joint cap; Catalan evidence shows these are the main avoidance channels.
- Spain has third-party reporting for most financial assets and CRS data; unlisted shares are valued at book value (or capitalised profits), so responses are likely closer to Scandinavian/Catalan than Colombian magnitudes.
- No Spanish causal study yet on the ITSGF itself or on emigration abroad of wealthy taxpayers.
