## Scope
Crime, legal status, enforcement (removals), border control and the politics of immigration. Labour-market and fiscal effects are in the companion notes.

## Mechanisms
- **Composition**: crime is concentrated among young men with low income; immigrant populations over-represent this group. Raw rates per resident therefore overstate any "nationality" effect.
- **Opportunity cost**: legal work raises the cost of crime; irregular status or bans on work lower it (economic model of crime).
- **Enforcement**: removals incapacitate some offenders but also deter crime reporting and cooperation with police; net effects can be null.
- **Measurement**: conviction statistics also reflect policing and procedure (hypothesis, not tested in the carded studies: e.g., differences in pre-trial detention or speedy trials for people without fixed address); offences tied to status (e.g., document fraud) exist only for foreigners.
- **Borders**: barriers raise crossing costs; migrants substitute routes (sea vs land, Canaries vs Mediterranean vs Balearics), smugglers adapt; deaths at sea can rise when routes lengthen.
- **Politics**: visible inflows raise salience and support for restrictive parties, especially where contact is limited (rural areas).

## Evidence by claim
**claim-imm-crime-raw-gap-composition (moderate).** In Spain the INE conviction statistics for 2025 report 15.3 convicted adults per 1,000 foreign-nationality residents aged 18+ vs 6.1 per 1,000 Spaniards (ratio 2.5), with no controls for age, sex, income or legal status (see es-dossier-inmigracion). Peer-reviewed Spanish evidence (Alonso-Borrego et al. 2012) finds the immigration-crime association is explained mainly by immigrants' human capital. In Italy legal status explains one half to two thirds of the legal-illegal gap.

**claim-imm-crime-causal-effect-small (moderate).** UK: asylum wave raised property crime modestly, A8 wave lowered it slightly, no violent-crime effect (Bell, Fasani & Machin 2013).

**claim-imm-crime-legal-status-reduces (moderate).** Italy: legal status reduced recidivism by about 50% (Mastrobuoni & Pinotti 2015). Implication for Spain: policies that increase the irregular stock (abolishing arraigo, no regularisations) could raise crime risk among that group; regularisations could lower it. No Spanish causal study on crime after the 2005 or 2026 regularisations was found.

**claim-imm-enf-removals-no-crime-reduction (moderate).** Secure Communities: no observable effect on the overall crime rate despite about 250,000 detentions (Miles & Cox 2014).

**claim-imm-border-barriers-displace (weak).** US wall: about 300,000 fewer migrants (one third of the 2005-2015 decline), with route and destination diversion (Allen et al. 2018, model-based). Spain: in 2025 total irregular arrivals fell 42.6% while sea arrivals to the Balearics rose 24.5%; in 2026 the Canary route rose again (Interior data in es-dossier-inmigracion). These are descriptive, not causal.

**claim-imm-pol-inflows-raise-restrictive-vote (moderate).** Denmark: refugee allocation increased anti-immigration party vote shares outside large cities (Dustmann et al. 2019). Attitudes driven by sociotropic cultural concerns (Hainmueller & Hopkins 2014).

## Open debates
- Size of property-crime effects when migrants have no legal work access, and how fast they fade with regularisation.
- Deterrence: whether tough border or removal policies reduce future inflows ("pull factors") versus displacement; Banco de España finds destination (pull) factors gaining weight in EU inflows, but policy-specific deterrence estimates for Spain are lacking.
- Magnet effects of regularisations: none detected for Spain 2005 in the short run (Elias et al. 2025); long-run expectation effects are not identified.
- Externalisation (cooperation with Morocco, Mauritania, Senegal) and its human-rights costs: no carded causal evidence.

## Transfer to Spain
- Spain-specific crime evidence is thin and mostly descriptive; INE raw rates should not be read as causal.
- Policies on the table (2026 programs): Vox proposes mass repatriation of irregular residents, "remigración", deportation for serious crimes and naval/military border measures; PP proposes expulsion for serious crimes and multi-recidivism, faster returns under the EU Return Regulation, longer detention in CIE, a points/quota system and a ban on mass regularisations. The international evidence speaks most directly to: (a) removals vs. crime (null), (b) legal status vs. crime (reduces), (c) border barriers (partial, displacement).
