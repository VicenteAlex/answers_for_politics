## Question
Do children of immigrants catch up economically with children of natives, and has this changed over time?

## Method and data
Linked father-son pairs (historical censuses and modern administrative data); rank-rank mobility comparing sons of immigrant vs. US-born fathers at the same point of the income distribution.

## Findings (abstract)
- Children of immigrants from nearly all sending countries have higher upward mobility than children of the US-born.
- The advantage is similar in the age of mass migration and today, despite changes in origins and policy.
- Part of the advantage comes from immigrant families locating in areas with better opportunities.

## Limitations
Descriptive/observational (graded C); US-specific labour market and naturalisation regime; sons only in historical data.

## Relevance
Evidence on second-generation outcomes. Spanish evidence on the second generation is thinner (the second generation of the 2000s wave is only now entering the labour market); see the gap noted in es-dossier-inmigracion.
