## Question
How did the increase in industrial robots between 1990 and 2007 affect employment and wages in US local labour markets?

## Method and data
Task-based model in which robots compete with labour. Exposure of each commuting zone = baseline industry employment shares × industry-level robot penetration, instrumented with robot adoption in European countries (shift-share IV). Controls for Chinese and Mexican imports, routine-job decline, offshoring, other IT and total capital.

## Findings (abstract)
- One more robot per thousand workers reduces the employment-to-population ratio by 0.2 percentage points and wages by 0.42% (published version; the 2017 working paper gave ranges of 0.18-0.34 pp and 0.25-0.5%).
- No differential pre-1990 trends in the most exposed areas; effects distinct from trade, routinisation, offshoring and other capital.

## Limitations
- Grade B: credible shift-share IV with pre-trend checks, but identification rests on exogeneity of baseline industry shares and on European adoption reflecting technology, not US demand shocks.
- Local (relative) effects; national general-equilibrium effects need extra assumptions.
- Period ends 2007; covers industrial robots, not AI.

## Relevance
Reference estimate for robot displacement in a low-protection labour market. Contrast with Germany (Dauth et al. 2021: no net job loss) and Spanish firms (Koch et al. 2021) suggests institutions and adjustment margins (hiring vs layoffs) shape outcomes. Spain's robot density is concentrated in automotive manufacturing.
