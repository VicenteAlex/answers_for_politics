## Question
Do the globally connected super-rich leave when their tax privileges are removed?

## Method and data
HMRC administrative data on non-doms (86% in the UK top 1% and 29% in the top 0.1% once overseas income is counted). The 2017 reform made long-stayers (15 of the last 20 years in the UK) deemed domiciled; comparison with non-doms not affected.

## Findings
- Net-of-average-tax rate of affected individuals fell by 18%.
- Central estimate: emigration rate +0.26 pp per 1% decline in net-of-tax rate; increases above 0.4 pp ruled out. Emigration responses were modest.
- Large average increases (>150%) in income reported and tax paid in the UK by the affected group.
- A related version (per search summaries) reports revenue gains of about GBP 3.2bn from abolishing the regime under similar elasticities; not verified here.

## Limitations
Working paper (not peer reviewed at the time of this card); long-stayers are less mobile by construction; income tax setting, not a net wealth tax.

## Relevance
Evidence that removing preferential regimes for the very rich need not trigger mass exit; relevant to Spain's debates on special regimes (e.g. impatriate regime) and on ITSGF emigration claims.
