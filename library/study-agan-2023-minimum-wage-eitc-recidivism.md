## Question
Do higher legal earnings opportunities (minimum wage, Earned Income Tax Credit) reduce re-offending by released prisoners?

## Method and data
Administrative prison release records across US states; variation in state minimum wages and state EITC top-ups at the time of release; outcome is return to prison.

## Findings (abstract)
- A $0.50 minimum wage increase reduces the probability of returning to prison within three years by 2.15% (relative), concentrated in property and drug crimes. (The 2018 working paper reported 2.8% within one year.)
- State EITCs reduce recidivism only for women.

## Limitations
- Ex-prisoners only; general-population effects can differ (see Fone, Sabia & Cesur 2023 for youth arrests).
- Effect is for modest US increases; employment losses for low-skilled workers would cut in the opposite direction.
- Recidivism measured as reincarceration, not all offending.

## Relevance
Mechanism "higher legal wage → lower returns to crime" applies to Spain's SMI increases (2018-2025) for people near the minimum-wage segment. With Fone et al. (2023) it shows the sign of the net effect depends on employment effects and on who is affected.
