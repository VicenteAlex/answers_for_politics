## Question
Do wealthy taxpayers move between Spanish regions in response to regional wealth-tax differences, and would coordination raise revenue?

## Method and data
After RDL 13/2011 reactivated the Impuesto sobre el Patrimonio, regions set their own parameters; Madrid kept a 100% tax credit (bonificación) while the rest levied positive rates. The authors use administrative tax records of wealth-tax filers to compare changes in reported residence (to Madrid vs elsewhere) before and after reintroduction, and simulate revenue under coordination.

## Findings
- Wealth-tax-induced mobility elasticities fall within the range of previous estimates for income taxes.
- Because movers also take their personal income tax base with them, regional losses in personal income tax revenue are about six times the direct wealth-tax losses.
- Madrid itself could obtain higher total regional revenue under a harmonized positive wealth-tax rate (gains from attracting movers are smaller than the revenue from taxing residents).

## Limitations
Reported residence may differ from real residence (fictitious moves); estimates refer to within-country mobility, not emigration abroad. Card based on the abstract.

## Relevance
Primary Spanish evidence on regional tax competition in the IP; directly relevant to the ITSGF's harmonizing rationale and to proposals for a state-level floor.
