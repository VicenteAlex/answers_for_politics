## Question
How have employment and wages across European occupations evolved in relation to exposure to AI and software?

## Method and data
EU Labour Force Survey occupational data (ISCO 3-digit) for 16 countries, 2011-2019; occupation-level exposure to AI and software (Webb-type and Felten-type measures); regressions of changes in employment shares and wages on exposure, by country and worker group. Also Banco de España Working Paper 2322 and ECB/CEPR versions.

## Findings (abstract)
- On average, employment shares increased in occupations more exposed to AI, particularly those with more young and skilled workers (consistent with skill-biased technical change).
- Heterogeneity across countries, but only very few show a decline in employment shares of occupations exposed to AI-enabled automation.
- Heterogeneity linked to the pace of technology diffusion and education, and to product-market regulation (competition) and employment protection laws.
- Little evidence of a relationship between wages and exposure.
- Spain's country-specific coefficient was not extracted here.

## Limitations
- Grade C: correlational, occupation-level; exposure measures predate generative AI; 2011-2019 period.
- The link to employment protection is a cross-country association, not a causal test.

## Relevance
The main peer-reviewed study including Spain. Co-authored by Banco de España economists (Jimeno). The suggestion that employment protection and competition shape outcomes speaks directly to whether Spanish labour institutions moderate impact.
