## Question
How much did physical border barriers reduce irregular migration, and how much did they simply displace it to other routes?

## Method and data
Reduced-form evidence on route and destination changes after wall segments were built, combined with a quantitative spatial equilibrium model of migration and US labour markets.

## Findings (abstract)
- The wall changed crossing routes, US destinations and the decision to migrate.
- Net migration reduction of about 300,000 people, roughly one third of the fall in Mexican migration between 2005 and 2015.
- Welfare/wage effects for US workers were very small: about +USD 9 per year for low-skill and -USD 21 per year for high-skill workers.

## Limitations
Working paper (not yet peer-reviewed in the version read); results partly model-based (graded C).

## Relevance
Evidence on route displacement and the limited aggregate labour-market payoff of border barriers. Spanish data show similar route shifts (e.g. Canary route falls in 2025 while Baleares rises; see es-dossier-inmigracion).
