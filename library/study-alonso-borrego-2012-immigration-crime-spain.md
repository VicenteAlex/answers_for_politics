## Question
Did the 1999-2009 immigration wave raise crime in Spain?

## Method and data
Province-level panel regressions of crime rates on the immigrant share, with controls and analysis by origin group (abstract; specification details not retrieved).

## Findings (abstract)
- Significant relationship between crime and immigration.
- The authors attribute it mainly to the characteristics of immigrant groups, in particular their amount and type of human capital, rather than to immigration per se.
- Spain's crime increase was smaller than in other European countries with comparable immigration.

## Limitations
- Associational design (graded C); no coefficients retrieved for this card.
- Recorded crime depends on policing intensity, which may itself respond to immigration.
- Does not separate legal from irregular status.

## Relevance
Main peer-reviewed Spain-specific evidence on immigration and crime. It is consistent with the international pattern that differences shrink once composition (age, sex, education, legal status) is accounted for, but it does find a positive raw/conditional association.
