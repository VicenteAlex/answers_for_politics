## Question
Is global tax evasion falling, and how much tax do the wealthiest pay?

## Method and data
Synthesis of research by the Observatory and partners: offshore wealth estimates (BIS banking statistics, leaks), corporate profit shifting, and studies with tax-administration data on billionaires' effective rates.

## Findings
- Before 2013 households held about 10% of world GDP in offshore financial wealth, mostly undeclared; today still about 10% of world GDP, but in the central scenario only about 25% evades taxation (untaxed offshore wealth = 3.2% of world GDP in 2022): a fall by a factor of about three.
- Global billionaires have effective personal tax rates equivalent to 0%-0.5% of their wealth (closer to 0.5% in the US, closer to 0% in France), partly because personal wealth-holding companies let owners avoid income tax on dividends.
- A 2% minimum tax on billionaires would raise nearly USD 250bn from fewer than 3,000 individuals (Table 2: 2,756 billionaires, USD 12.9tn wealth, USD 44bn personal tax currently paid; about USD 214bn net revenue in 2023).

## Limitations
Advocacy-oriented policy report by researchers who propose the tax; offshore estimates depend on assumptions about compliance; billionaire effective rates come from a few countries (no Spanish estimate); revenue figures assume limited behavioural responses.

## Relevance
Frequently cited source for "the richest pay little"; Spain's figures must be checked separately (see Fedea and AEAT data).
