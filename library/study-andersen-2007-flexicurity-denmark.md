## Question
What explains Denmark's low and stable unemployment, and is it attributable to the "flexicurity" combination of weak employment protection and generous unemployment insurance?

## Method and data
Narrative review of Danish institutions and reforms and of the evaluation literature on Danish active labour market policies (ALMP). No new causal estimation in the article itself. Only the abstract was checked for this card.

## Findings (abstract)
- Unemployment in Denmark is at a low and stable level, often attributed to the flexicurity model combining flexible hiring and firing rules with income security.
- The authors argue that, whatever the model's virtues, a low and stable unemployment rate is not automatically among them.
- They argue workfare (activation) policies have played an important role.
- Active labour market policies are resource-demanding; later reforms improved cost-effectiveness, but whether the spending is used efficiently remains open.

## Limitations
- Narrative review (grade D): attributes outcomes to institutions without a counterfactual; Danish performance also reflects macroeconomic conditions of the period.
- Predates the 2008-2009 recession, a stress test for the model not covered here.
- Quantitative details of the underlying evaluations were not checked.

## Relevance
"Flexicurity" is often proposed as a model for absorbing technological displacement: easy reallocation plus income support and retraining. This review cautions that its labour-market results depend on strict activation and costly ALMP, not on flexibility and benefits alone. Spain has a strongly dual contract structure, so transplanting the model for an AI shock would hinge on the activation component.
