## Question
Do mandated dismissal protections lower productivity by distorting firms' production choices?

## Method and data
Difference-in-differences using the staggered adoption, by US state courts between 1970 and 1999, of exceptions to employment-at-will (wrongful-discharge protections). Outcomes from Census Bureau establishment-level data: employment flows, firm entry, capital intensity and total factor productivity (TFP).

## Findings (abstract)
- Wrongful-discharge protections reduced employment flows and firm entry rates.
- Plants engaged in capital deepening and experienced declines in TFP, which the authors read as evidence of altered production techniques.
- Because of strong contemporaneous employment growth, the authors describe the findings as "suggestive but tentative".
- The abstract reports no point estimates; the size of effects is not recorded here.

## Limitations
- US court-made doctrines are a much milder form of protection than European statutory severance and procedures; magnitudes do not transfer directly.
- Timing of court decisions may correlate with state economic conditions.
- Mainly manufacturing establishments.

## Relevance
Within-country evidence that dismissal costs reduce worker and firm turnover and can shift input mix (more capital per worker). For an automation/AI shock, it suggests firing costs slow labour reallocation and may push firms toward capital-intensive adjustments; it says nothing about displaced workers' outcomes. For Spain, the direction is informative; the size is not.
