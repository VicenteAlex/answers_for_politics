## Question
Does employment protection legislation (EPL) change which technologies firms adopt, and through that channel, aggregate productivity growth?

## Method and data
Reduced-form regressions on a country-industry panel (EUKLEMS) relating the size and productivity growth of "high-risk" sectors (sectors with high dispersion of firm outcomes) to EPL stringency. A two-sector search-and-matching model in which firms choose between a safe technology and a risky one with higher expected productivity; firing costs make shedding workers after a bad draw expensive. The model is calibrated to aggregate, industry and micro data and simulated for cross-country EPL differences and the rise in firm-performance variance linked to ICT adoption since the mid-1990s.

## Findings (abstract)
- High-risk sectors, which contribute strongly to aggregate productivity growth, are relatively smaller and grow more slowly in high-EPL countries.
- In the model, strict EPL lowers the option value of risky technologies, so high-EPL countries gain less from new risky technologies.
- Regressions on actual and model-simulated data give qualitatively similar results; the mechanism is consistent with the EU's productivity slowdown relative to the US since the mid-1990s.
- The abstract gives no headline effect size; magnitudes depend on the calibration.

## Limitations
- Cross-country EPL variation is not randomly assigned; identification of the reduced-form part rests on cross-country/industry correlations.
- Quantitative effects are only as good as the model's assumptions (grade C).

## Relevance
Directly relevant to a general-purpose technology shock such as AI: the mechanism predicts that high firing costs for permanent workers (Spain's historical EPL on indefinite contracts) slow adoption of risky, high-variance technologies and the reallocation they require. It speaks to the speed of adoption and productivity, not to the welfare of displaced workers.
