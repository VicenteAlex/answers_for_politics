## Question
Does the stringency of dismissal regulation affect productivity growth?

## Method and data
Difference-in-differences across countries and industries: annual OECD EPL indicators interacted with an industry's "natural" layoff propensity (industries where layoff restrictions are more likely to bind versus those where they are less likely to). Industry-level productivity data, 1982-2003. The design is meant to control for unobserved factors common to all industries within a country; the paper includes robustness checks, including on endogeneity.

## Findings (abstract)
- Mandatory dismissal regulations have a depressing impact on productivity growth in industries where layoff restrictions are more likely to be binding.
- No evidence of a productivity effect of regulations concerning temporary contracts. The authors conclude that partial reforms of temporary contracts are unlikely to substitute for comprehensive reform of dismissal restrictions.
- Point estimates are in the paper; they are not reproduced here because they were not checked against the full text.

## Limitations
- Aggregate EPL indices summarise heterogeneous rules (severance, procedures, courts).
- Identifies a relative effect (binding vs non-binding industries), not the aggregate level effect.

## Relevance
Highly relevant to Spain's dual market: it suggests that liberalising temporary contracts (Spain's 1984 route) does not deliver the productivity gains associated with lower dismissal costs on regular contracts. For an AI/automation shock, binding dismissal rules slow within-industry reallocation, which matters more when the shock is structural rather than temporary.
