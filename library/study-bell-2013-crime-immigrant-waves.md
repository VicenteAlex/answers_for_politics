## Question
Do large immigrant inflows raise local crime, and does it depend on the migrants' labour-market prospects?

## Method and data
Area-level panel of crime rates in England and Wales; asylum-seeker inflows were driven by the national dispersal policy (quasi-random allocation), A8 inflows instrumented with prior settlement patterns.

## Findings (abstract)
- Asylum wave (limited legal access to work): "a modest but significant rise in property crime".
- A8 wave (full labour-market access): "a small negative impact" on property crime.
- No effect on violent crime in either wave.
- Interpretation: labour-market opportunities of migrants matter for property crime.

## Limitations
- Recorded crime and area-level data: cannot attribute offences to individuals.
- Effect sizes not retrieved for this card.

## Relevance
Supports an economic-opportunity mechanism: restrictions on legal work (as for asylum seekers awaiting decisions, or irregular migrants) can raise property crime. Directly relevant to Spain's debates on work authorisation for asylum seekers and irregular residents.
