## Question
Is worker voice (employee representation, ER, at the workplace) associated with lower exposure of jobs to automation?

## Method and data
An evolutionary game-theoretic model of job design and workplace governance predicts two stable conventions: ER combined with low-automation-risk jobs, and no ER with automation-prone tasks. Empirics: pooled EWCS 2010 and 2015 (roughly 44,000 observations per wave); a task-based job automation risk measure; OLS/probit with individual and firm controls; recursive bivariate probit using the industry-country share of ER workplaces as an instrument; robot-stock data (IFR) for a subsample.

## Findings
- ER presence is associated with a 3.5 percentage point lower probability of being exposed to high automation risk after controls (about half the reduction associated with computer use at work).
- The bivariate probit marginal effect is a 13 percentage point reduction.
- The negative association is larger for less educated employees.
- The paper relates the result to Nordic experience combining codetermination with job enrichment programmes.

## Limitations
- Cross-sectional correlation; the instrument (industry-country ER share) is open to exclusion-restriction concerns. Grade C.
- Automation risk is a task-content measure, not realised displacement.
- Figures are from the IZA working paper; the published version may differ.

## Relevance
Spain has elected works councils (comités de empresa) and union delegates. The study suggests, without establishing causality, that firms with worker representation organise jobs with fewer automatable tasks; whether representation protects workers from an AI shock or only sorts them into less exposed jobs is unresolved.
