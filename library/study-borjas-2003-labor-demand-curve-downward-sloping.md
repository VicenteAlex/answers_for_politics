## Question
Does immigration lower wages of competing workers when competition is measured at the national level within education-experience cells (avoiding the bias from natives moving across cities)?

## Method and data
Panel of education x experience cells for the US national labour market, 1960-2000; regresses cell wages on the immigrant share of the cell, with fixed effects.

## Findings
- "A 10 percent increase in supply reduces wages by 3 to 4 percent" for the competing group (abstract).

## Limitations
- Identifies a relative (partial) effect between cells, not the total effect on native wages once capital and demand adjust (Dustmann, Schönberg & Stuhler 2016).
- Assumes immigrants compete with natives of the same measured education and experience; occupational downgrading of immigrants breaks this assumption.
- Not a natural experiment; immigrant allocation across cells is not random. Graded C.

## Relevance
Main source for the view that low-skilled natives can lose from low-skill immigration. In Spain the relevant comparison is with the large 2000-2008 and 2022-2026 inflows into low-wage services and construction.
