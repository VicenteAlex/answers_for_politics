## Question
Did Card (1990) miss a wage effect because he pooled workers who did not compete with the Marielitos?

## Method and data
Same natural experiment, but matching skills: at least 60% of the Marielitos were high-school dropouts, so Borjas focuses on the wages of non-Hispanic men aged 25-59 without a high-school diploma, comparing Miami with several control groups (including Card's) and synthetic-control-style comparisons.

## Findings
- Wages of high-school dropouts in Miami fell "dramatically", by 10 to 30% relative to comparison cities (abstract).
- Implied wage elasticity with respect to the number of workers of -0.5 to -1.5.

## Limitations
- Very small cells (a few dozen observations per year in Miami for the chosen group), so estimates are noisy.
- Clemens and Hunt (2019, not carded) and Peri & Yasenov (2019) argue the drop coincides with a change in CPS coverage of black workers and is sensitive to sample choices. The debate is unresolved.
- Abstract-level reading for this card.

## Relevance
The strongest single-episode evidence for sizeable short-run wage losses among the lowest-skilled natives. Its relevance to Spain depends on how closely recent arrivals substitute for low-skilled natives (occupational downgrading of immigrants makes them closer substitutes than formal education suggests: see study-dustmann-2016-why-studies-differ).
