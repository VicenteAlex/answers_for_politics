## Question
How large and how persistent is the effect of a second-generation rent cap on rents, and who benefits?

## Method and data
Triple-difference event study on rental offer prices, comparing regulated and unregulated dwellings and municipalities before and after adoption of the 2015 rent brake.

## Findings
- Rent control lowered rental prices by up to 5% for all dwellings and up to 9% for specific dwelling types.
- The effect fades roughly one year after implementation, with essentially no effect afterwards.
- Benefits accrued mostly in areas with high-income households, missing the target group.
- The quality of offered dwellings declined under the regulation.

## Limitations
Asking rents in listings, not contract rents; weak enforcement of the German cap (no ex-ante registration) may explain the fading effect.

## Relevance
Shows that the effectiveness of second-generation caps depends on enforcement and can be short-lived. Catalonia's 2020 and 2024 systems include ad and contract disclosure of the reference price, so enforcement differs.
