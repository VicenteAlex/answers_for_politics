## Question
Is generative AI already reducing employment, and for whom?

## Method and data
High-frequency ADP payroll data (millions of US workers). Occupations ranked by AI exposure (Eloundou et al. measures and Anthropic usage data split into automative vs augmentative use). Regressions of employment by age group × exposure quintile with firm-time fixed effects to absorb firm-level shocks (e.g. interest rates).

## Findings
- Aug 2025 version: early-career workers (22-25) in the most AI-exposed occupations had a 13% relative employment decline after controlling for firm-level shocks; older workers in the same occupations and less-exposed groups were stable or growing.
- Nov 2025 revision: 16% (data to Oct 2025). Aug 2026 revision: employment of 22-25 year olds in exposed occupations 19% below its counterfactual (data to June 2026).
- Declines concentrated where AI use mainly substitutes for tasks; where it complements, employment is flat or rising.
- The authors call these early, descriptive indicators, not causal estimates.
- The intermediate figures (13%, 16%) come from secondary summaries and the authors' notes; the 19% from the official page.

## Limitations
- Grade C: observational with fixed effects; the timing coincides with post-2022 interest-rate rises and tech-sector corrections, a disputed confounder.
- Not peer-reviewed; headline estimate changed across revisions.
- ADP clients are not a random sample of US firms.

## Relevance
Points to the same margin as robots in Germany (Dauth et al. 2021): fewer entry-level hires rather than layoffs. Contrasts with Danish null results (Humlum & Vestergaard 2025). No Spanish equivalent with payroll microdata identified.
