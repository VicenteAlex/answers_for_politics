## Question
How does a generative-AI conversational assistant affect worker productivity, and for whom?

## Method and data
Staggered introduction of a GPT-based assistant across 5,172 customer-support agents (Stanford Digital Economy Lab description) at one firm; event-study/difference-in-differences on issues resolved per hour, resolution rate and customer sentiment.

## Findings
- QJE abstract: productivity (issues resolved per hour) increases 15% on average, with substantial heterogeneity.
- Less experienced and lower-skilled workers improve both speed and quality; the most experienced see small speed gains and small quality declines.
- 2023 working paper: 14% average, 34% for novice and low-skilled workers (the 34% was not verified in the published abstract).
- Suggestive evidence that the model spreads best practices of top workers.

## Limitations
- Grade B: credible staggered rollout, but one firm, one occupation, early model.
- Measures task productivity, not employment: it says nothing directly on whether the firm hires fewer agents.

## Relevance
Main evidence that gen-AI compresses skill gaps within an occupation. Productivity gains do not settle displacement: the employment effect depends on demand elasticity and firm decisions, which institutions can shape.
