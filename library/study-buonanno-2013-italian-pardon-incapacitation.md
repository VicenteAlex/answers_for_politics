## Question
How much crime does incarceration prevent by incapacitation, and does the effect shrink as more people are imprisoned?

## Method and data
The August 2006 pardon released about one third of Italy's prisoners within days. Three sources of variation: (1) the discontinuous break in national crime rates at the release; (2) the subsequent return of the incarceration rate to its steady state; (3) provincial differences in releases by pardoned inmates' province of residence.

## Findings
- Break in national series: 14-18 crimes per prison-year served; 17-21 after adjusting for the deterrence effects estimated by Drago, Galbiati & Vertova (2009).
- Dynamic adjustment path: larger estimates, 22-46.
- Effects concentrated in theft and robbery.
- Crime-preventing effects of incarceration diminish as the incarceration rate rises.

## Limitations
- Reported crimes; the incapacitation estimate depends on the composition of released inmates (short residual sentences).
- A one-off mass release; not directly comparable to sentence reductions for specific offences.

## Relevance
Releasing prisoners raises property crime in the short run, by an amount proportional to the number released and their offending rates. Spain's 2022-2023 LO 10/2022 sentence reductions led to on the order of a hundred early releases of sex offenders per CGPJ tallies (two orders of magnitude fewer than the ~22,000 released in Italy), so any effect on aggregate street crime is expected to be undetectable in national statistics; the relevant outcome is re-offending by the specific released people.
