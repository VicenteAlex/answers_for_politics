## Question
Which active labour market programmes (ALMPs) work, for whom, and over what horizon?

## Method and data
Meta-analysis of estimates from over 200 recent evaluation studies. Estimates classified by programme type, participant group and three post-programme horizons (short, medium, long run). Regression models for the estimated effect size (studies measuring employment probability) and for the sign and significance of estimates (all studies).

## Findings (abstract)
- Average impacts are close to zero in the short run but become more positive 2-3 years after completion of the programme.
- The time profile varies by programme type, with larger average gains for programmes that emphasise human capital accumulation.
- Larger impacts for women and for participants who enter from long-term unemployment.
- ALMPs are more likely to show positive impacts in a recession.
- Exact effect sizes are in the paper and are not reproduced here.

## Limitations
Pools experimental and non-experimental evaluations; publication bias and heterogeneous outcome measures; little evidence on general-equilibrium (displacement) effects among non-participants.

## Relevance
For an AI/automation displacement shock, the time profile matters: retraining is unlikely to show gains within a year but can pay off after 2-3 years, so judging programmes on short-run placement rates (common in Spain's public employment services) undervalues human-capital programmes. Positive impacts in recessions and for the long-term unemployed suggest a role when reallocation is slow.
