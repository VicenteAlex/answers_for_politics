## Question
How exposed is employment to AI across countries, and is that exposure likely to complement or substitute labour?

## Method and data
AI Occupational Exposure index (Felten, Raj & Seamans 2021) combined with a "complementarity" index (social, ethical, physical context of the job) applied to occupational employment shares by country; model-based scenarios for productivity and inequality.

## Findings (executive summary)
- Almost 40% of global employment is exposed to AI.
- Advanced economies: about 60% of jobs exposed; 27% in high exposure-high complementarity (HEHC) and 33% in high exposure-low complementarity (HELC).
- Emerging markets 40%, low-income countries 26%; emerging markets 16% HEHC, 24% HELC.
- Policy: protect and retrain displaced workers, reallocation policies and safety nets; pension and unemployment-insurance generosity can amplify re-employment frictions for older workers.
- No Spain-specific figure in the note.

## Limitations
- Grade D: exposure plus a constructed complementarity index; neither is an observed outcome. Inherits AIOE limits.
- Staff note, not peer-reviewed; views of staff, not necessarily the IMF Board.

## Relevance
Frequently quoted "40%/60%" figures. For Spain, the AE average is only indicative. The note explicitly makes labour-market institutions (UI, pensions, retraining) part of the transmission.
