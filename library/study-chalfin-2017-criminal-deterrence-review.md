## Question
What does the economics literature say about how police, sanctions and labour-market opportunities affect crime?

## Method and data
Narrative review of mostly quasi-experimental studies on police, the certainty and severity of punishment, and legitimate labour-market opportunities.

## Findings (abstract)
- Crime is responsive to police presence (certainty of apprehension).
- Crime is responsive to the availability of attractive legitimate labour-market opportunities.
- Far less evidence that crime responds to the severity of criminal sanctions (sentence length).

## Limitations
- Narrative, not a formal meta-analysis; graded A here because it synthesises a large body of causal studies, but conclusions rest on predominantly US evidence.
- Distinguishes deterrence from incapacitation only partly; incapacitation effects of imprisonment are a separate literature (e.g. Buonanno & Raphael 2013).

## Relevance
Framework for the Spanish question: police deployment and legal job opportunities are the policy levers with the clearest causal links to crime; harsher or milder sentencing changes deterrence little, though releases still matter through incapacitation.
