## Question
What is the causal effect of police numbers on crime, and are US cities staffed below the socially optimal level?

## Method and data
City-year panel of police employment and crime (UCR), 1960-2010. Uses a second, independent measure of police (Annual Survey of Government) to correct for measurement error in the main series, with instrumental-variables estimates.

## Findings
- Measurement-error-corrected elasticities are similar in size to quasi-experimental estimates but much more precise.
- Working-paper estimates (IV with state-year effects): violent crime about -0.35, property crime about -0.17; best guess for murder -0.67 ± 0.48.
- Police reduce violent crime more than property crime; the welfare analysis concludes US cities are substantially underpoliced.
- (Published-version numbers may differ slightly from the 2015 working paper.)

## Limitations
- US cities, where violent crime and gun homicide are far higher than in Spain; welfare conclusions depend on US cost-of-crime figures.
- Identification relies on the measurement-error correction rather than an exogenous shock.

## Relevance
Gives the order of magnitude for police effects (elasticities of -0.1 to -0.7 depending on crime type). Any assessment of Spanish crime trends 2016-2026 must account for changes in police staffing and deployment alongside social policies.
