## Question
Does a conditional cash transfer to poor families reduce crime?

## Method and data
School-level data on Bolsa Família coverage combined with geo-referenced police crime records in São Paulo. Identification from the 2008 extension of the benefit to 16- and 17-year-olds, interacted with each school's initial share of eligible students (instrumental variables).

## Findings (abstract)
- A robust and significant negative effect of Bolsa Família coverage on crime near schools.
- Incapacitation (time in school) does not appear to be the main channel; income or peer-group changes are.
- Secondary summaries quote a reduction of about 21% (either in robbery/theft near schools or as a share of the city's crime decline); the exact quantity was not verified against the paper and is not used here.

## Limitations
- One city, a middle-income country with much higher baseline crime than Spain.
- Conditional transfer tied to school attendance; differs from an unconditional minimum income.
- Local crime around schools; displacement not fully assessed.

## Relevance
Best-identified evidence that a means-tested family cash transfer can lower property crime. External validity to Spain's Ingreso Mínimo Vital (2020) is limited: lower crime baseline, different benefit design and recipients.
