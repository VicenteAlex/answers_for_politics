## Question
Did the 2021 reform, which restricted fixed-term contracts and promoted open-ended variants (notably the fijo discontinuo), reduce job instability, or only the contractual label?

## Method and data
New daily administrative data on all Social Security affiliation creations and destructions (Ministry of Inclusion, Social Security and Migration). Before/after comparisons of creation and destruction calendars ("calendar effects"), job duration and survival, and one-year transition matrices across contract types. No formal counterfactual.

## Findings (working paper, December 2023; published abstract consistent)
- EPA temporary employment rate fell from 25.0% to around 17% (EU average 12% in 2022, Eurostat).
- Permanent workers still permanent one year later: 90.0%, hardly changed.
- Temporary workers on a permanent contract one year later: around 18% before vs 33.16% after; temporary to fijo discontinuo: 1.5% to 9.16%.
- Fijo discontinuo workers moving to an ordinary permanent contract: 3.17% to 17.14%.
- Job duration and short-term transitions did not improve significantly; mortality of ordinary permanent contracts rose, many ending in the trial period.

## Limitations
Descriptive before/after evidence (grade D); post-reform period overlaps with post-COVID recovery and high inflation. Numbers quoted from the 2023 working paper; the 2025 journal version may differ.

## Relevance
Shows that relabelling contracts does not by itself change turnover. For an AI shock, displacement risk will fall on "empirically" unstable jobs regardless of contract type; instruments targeting workers (UI, training) may matter more than contract rules.
