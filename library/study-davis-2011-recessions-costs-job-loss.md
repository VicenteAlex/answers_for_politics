## Question
How large are the lifetime earnings costs of job loss, and do they depend on labour-market conditions at the time of displacement?

## Method and data
Longitudinal US Social Security earnings records, 1974-2008. Men displaced in mass-layoff events are followed over time; earnings losses are cumulated over 20 years after displacement and discounted. Losses compared across displacement years by the national unemployment rate. A Mortensen-Pissarides search model with on-the-job search is tested against the estimates.

## Findings (abstract)
- Displaced when the national unemployment rate is below 6 percent: average present-value loss of 1.4 years of pre-displacement earnings.
- Displaced when unemployment exceeds 8 percent: 2.8 years.
- Present values at a 5% annual discount rate over 20 years after displacement.
- The search model's present-value losses are one-fourth as large as observed and vary little with aggregate conditions, unlike the data.

## Limitations
Men only; mass-layoff definition excludes small firms and individual dismissals; US institutions (weak UI). Brookings Papers are editor-reviewed with discussants, recorded here as not peer reviewed in the journal sense.

## Relevance
The cost of displacement roughly doubles when it happens in a slack labour market. If an AI/automation shock coincides with high unemployment, as is typical in Spain, losses for displaced stable workers would be at the high end. This strengthens the case for smoothing the timing of separations (e.g. STW in downturns) and for reemployment support, while STW cannot avoid structural losses.
