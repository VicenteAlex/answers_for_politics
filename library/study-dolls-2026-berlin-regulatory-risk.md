## Question
Do rent caps and threats of further intervention (expropriation referendum) affect house prices relative to rents, ownership structure and construction, even after the cap is repealed?

## Method and data
Hedonic difference-in-differences on about 2.3 million rental and 1.1 million sale listings in the 14 largest German cities (2017-2024), Berlin vs the other 13, relative to 2018Q3. Outcome: price-to-rent ratio. A newspaper-based housing-policy-uncertainty index is built for 2016-2023. Only the column was read; the working paper was not opened.

## Findings
- While the rent cap was in force (2020-2021), Berlin's price-to-rent ratio stood above its counterfactual.
- After the cap was struck down, the ratio declined and from late 2023 through 2024 stabilised about 15% below the counterfactual (10-15% three years after repeal); the authors attribute this to perceived regulatory risk.
- Large housing companies cut asking rents more during the cap, later sold (including to the Land Berlin) and accepted lower sale prices; private landlords raised rents 15-20% above trend after repeal.
- Approved new construction and modernisation in Berlin fell relative to comparable cities, driven by the corporate sector.
- Short-run cap distortions (fewer listings, more intensive search, lower mobility) had largely faded by 2023.

## Limitations
Not peer reviewed; listing data; Berlin-specific combination of a strict cap and an expropriation referendum. The rise of the price-to-rent ratio during the cap mixes falling capped rents with sale prices; it is not a direct estimate of sale-price effects.

## Relevance
Most direct recent evidence on the link between rent regulation and the sale market: during the cap, the sale market looked relatively stronger than the rental market (consistent with substitution toward sale), but afterwards regulatory risk lowered valuations and construction. Shows that the sign of the effect on prices can differ by horizon.
