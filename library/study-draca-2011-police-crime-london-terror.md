## Question
Does more police presence reduce crime?

## Method and data
The July 2005 terror attacks led to a large, temporary redeployment of police to central London. Difference-in-differences (central vs outer London boroughs) and instrumental variables using the redeployment as an instrument for police activity.

## Findings (abstract)
- Crime fell significantly in central relative to outer London during the redeployment.
- Elasticity of crime with respect to police: approximately -0.3 to -0.4.

## Limitations
- Short-run, highly visible deployment in a dense city centre; effects of permanent staffing changes may differ.
- Possible spatial displacement and changes in reporting during a security alert.

## Relevance
European evidence that visible police presence reduces street crime (mainly property and street crimes). For Spain, police staffing changes (Policía Nacional, Guardia Civil, regional and local police) are a candidate explanation for crime trends that is independent of social or labour policies.
