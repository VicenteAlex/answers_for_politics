## Question
Do longer expected sentences deter re-offending?

## Method and data
Under the July 2006 Italian Collective Clemency Bill, released inmates who re-offend must serve the pardoned residual sentence on top of the new one. The residual sentence varies across inmates, creating variation in expected punishment. Unique dataset on post-release behaviour of former inmates.

## Findings (abstract)
- Each month less served, offset by one month more of expected sentence for future crimes, lowers the probability of recidivism by 0.16 percentage points.
- Implied elasticity of recidivism with respect to expected punishment: -0.74 over a 7-month period.

## Limitations
- Former inmates only; short follow-up.
- Combines two shifts (less time served, more expected sentence), so it identifies the deterrent response to a conditional sentence, not a pure sentence-length effect.

## Relevance
Shows that the design of releases matters: early release combined with a suspended "sword of Damocles" sentence deterred re-offending. Spain's LO 10/2022 reductions were unconditional recalculations (favourable retroactive application of the new penalty ranges), without this conditional component.
