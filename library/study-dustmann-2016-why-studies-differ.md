## Question
Why do estimates of immigration's effect on native wages range from clearly negative (national skill-cell studies) to about zero (spatial studies)?

## Method and data
Conceptual review using the canonical CES labour-demand model; classifies studies by the variation they use and by the parameter identified.

## Findings
- Skill-cell and "mixture" approaches identify relative effects (one skill group vs. another); spatial approaches can identify total effects on a group. The numbers are "not comparable".
- When native labour-supply elasticities differ across groups, relative wage effects are hard to interpret.
- "Downgrading" (immigrants working below their measured skills) biases relative-effect estimates but not total-effect ones; the authors argue total-effect designs answer the policy question better.

## Limitations
Review, not new estimates. Graded A here as an authoritative methodological synthesis, not as causal evidence by itself.

## Relevance
Key to reading any Spanish or international number: ask whether it is a relative or total effect and which native group is meant. In Spain, recent immigrants are concentrated in low-wage occupations regardless of education, which makes downgrading relevant.
