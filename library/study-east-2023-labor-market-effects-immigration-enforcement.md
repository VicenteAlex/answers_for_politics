## Question
Does removing undocumented workers through police-based enforcement raise employment and wages of native workers?

## Method and data
Difference-in-differences exploiting the staggered county-level activation of Secure Communities (fingerprint checks of arrestees against immigration databases, leading to detention and deportation).

## Findings (abstract; magnitudes not retrieved)
- Employment of likely undocumented immigrants fell, through deportations and changes in behaviour of those who stayed.
- Employment and hourly wages of US-born workers also fell (male citizens in particular in the working-paper version).
- Mechanism proposed: complementarity; higher labour costs reduce job creation, and lower consumption reduces local demand.

## Limitations
- US-specific programme; identifying the undocumented in survey data relies on proxies.
- Effect sizes per removed worker not verified here.

## Relevance
Direct evidence against the expectation that large-scale removals raise natives' employment; applies to proposals of mass "repatriation" or "remigración" (Vox 2026 program), though Spain's labour market institutions differ.
