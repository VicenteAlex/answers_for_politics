## Question
What happens to labour markets and public finances when undocumented immigrants receive work permits?

## Method and data
The 2005 Spanish regularisation (about 600,000 people regularised according to the abstract; the KB event es-event-2005-05-07-regularizacion-inmigrantes records close to 700,000 applications) was conditional on an employment contract and prior registration in the padrón (background, not from the abstract). Variation across provinces in the share of regularised immigrants; comparisons of non-EU (eligible) with EU (not needing it) immigrants to test magnet effects; Social Security affiliation and tax data.

## Findings (abstract)
- No evidence of magnet effects (new inflows attracted by the policy).
- Formal employment of immigrants increased; natives' employment was not affected.
- Informal employment of both native and immigrant low-skilled workers decreased.
- Tax revenues rose by around EUR 4,000 per regularised immigrant, "with no evidence of increased public expenditures".
- The policy increased labour-market opportunities for immigrants.

## Limitations
- One episode, during an economic boom (2005); effects in a slack labour market may differ.
- The magnet-effect test compares groups whose migration costs and rights differ; long-run expectation effects (future regularisations anticipated) are hard to identify.
- Abstract-level reading.

## Relevance
Most directly relevant causal evidence for evaluating the 2026 extraordinary regularisation (es-measure-rd-316-2026-regularizacion-extraordinaria) and for proposals to ban regularisations (Vox and PP 2026 programs).
