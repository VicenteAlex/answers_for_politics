## Question
Which US occupations and what share of tasks are exposed to large language models (LLMs) and LLM-powered software?

## Method and data
Rubric of task "exposure" (whether an LLM could cut task time by at least half at equal quality), applied to O*NET tasks by human annotators and by GPT-4; aggregated to occupations and weighted by employment. Authors are affiliated with OpenAI and the University of Pennsylvania.

## Findings
- arXiv abstract (v1/v2): around 80% of the US workforce could have at least 10% of their work tasks affected, and approximately 19% may see at least 50% of their tasks impacted.
- Later arXiv version: with an LLM alone about 15% of all worker tasks could be completed significantly faster at the same quality; with LLM-powered software, 47-56% of tasks.
- Higher-wage occupations tend to be more exposed.
- The exact figures in the shorter Science (2024) version could not be verified; numbers above are from the arXiv versions.

## Limitations
- Grade D: exposure is a technical-feasibility rating, not an observed labour-market outcome; exposure ≠ automation ≠ job loss (it can mean augmentation).
- GPT-4 rating its own capabilities; authors employed by the model developer (potential interest).
- US occupational structure.

## Relevance
Widely used to map exposure; Spanish exposure estimates (e.g. Funcas 2026) borrow its parameters. Says nothing about timing or about how labour institutions mediate effects.
