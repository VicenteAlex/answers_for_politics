## Question
What are the costs of net wealth taxes in Europe?

## Method and data
Descriptive policy analysis using OECD revenue statistics, country cases, press reports and the publisher's own economic modelling.

## Findings (as argued by the author)
- Repeals: Austria (1994), Denmark and Germany (1997), Netherlands (2001), Finland, Iceland, Luxembourg (2006), Sweden (2007), France (2018, replaced by a real-estate wealth tax). Remaining in Europe: Norway, Spain, Switzerland.
- Revenue in 2022: 0.19% of GDP (Spain) to 1.19% (Switzerland); 0.51% to 4.35% of total tax revenue.
- Spain's solidarity tax: government expected over EUR 1.5bn, collected about EUR 0.6bn. (Note: Hacienda's EUR 1.5bn figure assumed all regions applied a 100% IP credit; the ITSGF is net of IP paid, so the comparison is not like-for-like.)
- Claims of emigration and capital flight (Norway, Sweden, Spain/Portugal) rest on press reports; growth costs rest on Tax Foundation model simulations.

## Limitations
Advocacy-oriented think tank; causal claims rely on anecdotes and own models rather than quasi-experimental evidence; does not engage with studies finding small real effects (Ring 2025; Jakobsen et al. 2024).

## Relevance
Representative statement of the critical position used by opponents of IP/ITSGF in Spain; its factual revenue data are usable, its causal claims are weak evidence.
