## Question
Do general amnesty programs reduce crime among immigrants at the aggregate (regional) level?

## Method and data
Cross-regional and time variation in the number of immigrants legalized by repeated Italian amnesties, 1990-2005. Endogeneity addressed by instrumenting the actual number legalized with predictions from past amnesty applications and residential choices of documented and undocumented immigrants.

## Findings (abstract)
- In the year following an amnesty, regions with a higher share of immigrants legalized saw a greater decline in non-EU immigrant crime rates.
- The effect is statistically significant but relatively small and not persistent.
- No evidence of crime displacement to EU immigrants or Italians.

## Limitations
- Aggregate crime reports by nationality; legal status of offenders not always observed.
- Short-run effect; cannot rule out that amnesties influence future irregular inflows.

## Relevance
Aggregate counterpart to Pinotti (2017): individual-level effects are large, but area-level effects of a general amnesty are small and transitory. Suggests Spain's 2026 regularisation should not be expected to produce large, lasting changes in aggregate street crime in either direction.
