## Question
What are the welfare effects of zoning changes, rent control, vouchers and tax credits once risk, risk aversion and incomplete markets are taken into account?

## Method and data
Dynamic stochastic spatial equilibrium model with endogenous house prices, rents, construction, labour supply, income and wealth inequality, location within the city and migration between cities. Calibrated to the New York MSA.

## Findings
- Affordability policies carry substantial insurance value but change aggregate housing and labour supply and cause misallocation (abstract).
- Policies that improve access to this insurance, especially for the neediest households, produce substantial net welfare gains (abstract).
- Working-paper versions report that relaxing zoning in Manhattan yields large welfare gains and that shrinking the existing rent-control system hurts current low-income beneficiaries; better targeting amplifies benefits (secondary summaries; not verified in the published text).

## Limitations
Results depend on calibration and model assumptions (risk aversion, targeting, construction response). Single US city.

## Relevance
Main theoretical counterweight to purely efficiency-based critiques: rent regulation can raise welfare as insurance if well targeted, even with supply costs. For Spain, Ley 12/2023 caps are not income-targeted, which the model suggests weakens the insurance case.
