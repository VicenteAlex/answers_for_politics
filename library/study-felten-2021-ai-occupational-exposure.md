## Question
How can exposure of occupations, industries and regions to AI be measured consistently?

## Method and data
Links ten common AI applications (from the Electronic Frontier Foundation AI progress data) to 52 O*NET occupational abilities, weighted by each ability's prevalence and importance in the occupation; aggregated to industries (AIIE) and US counties (AIGE). Validation against other measures.

## Findings (abstract and secondary summaries)
- Provides the AIOE, AIIE and AIGE datasets and proposes firm-level uses.
- Highest-exposure industries include financial services, accounting, insurance and legal services (secondary summary).
- The abstract reports no analysis of employment or wage outcomes.

## Limitations
- Grade D: exposure index, not an outcome study; it measures overlap with AI capabilities, not substitution vs complementarity.
- Pre-LLM AI applications (the 2023 follow-up by the same authors adapts it to language models; not a peer-reviewed paper and not carded here).

## Relevance
The AIOE is the base of the IMF exposure figures (Cazzaniga et al. 2024) and of Funcas's 2026 projection for Spain (adapted to CNO-11). Its limits therefore carry into those numbers.
