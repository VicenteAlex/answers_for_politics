## Question
Do minimum wage increases reduce crime, as argued by the 2016 US Council of Economic Advisers?

## Method and data
State-level minimum wage variation in the US, 1998-2016, difference-in-differences on Uniform Crime Reports arrest data by age; supplementary analysis of workers bound by minimum wage increases in the National Longitudinal Survey of Youth 1997 (and CPS in the working-paper version).

## Findings (abstract)
- No evidence that minimum wage increases reduce arrests.
- A 1% minimum wage increase is associated with a 0.2-0.3% increase in property-crime arrests among 16-24-year-olds, driven by larceny.
- No effect on violent-crime arrests; little effect on drug crime or among older individuals (working paper).
- Mechanism attributed to reduced employment of workers bound by the minimum wage. The authors estimate a $15 federal minimum wage could generate about 309,000 additional larcenies.

## Limitations
- Arrests, not offences; reporting and policing intensity may differ.
- US minimum wage increases are mostly small relative to the median wage; effects of very large increases are extrapolations.
- Contrasts with Agan & Makowsky (2023), who find lower recidivism for released prisoners: the sign may depend on the population (youth with low attachment vs. ex-prisoners seeking work) and on employment effects.

## Relevance
Spain raised the SMI by about 54% in 2018-2025 (22% in 2019 alone). The crime effect of a minimum wage depends on whether it reduces youth employment (more property crime) or raises legal earnings of those who keep jobs (less crime). Spanish evidence on the employment effects of the 2019 increase is therefore the key link; no study measures its effect on street crime.
