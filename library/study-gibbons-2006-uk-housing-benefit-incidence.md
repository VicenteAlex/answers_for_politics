## Question
How is the incidence of a means-tested rent subsidy split between tenants and landlords?

## Method and data
Difference-in-differences exploiting 1996 and 1997 reforms that reduced the maximum Housing Benefit payable, applied only to new claimants; existing claimants form the control group. Uses two mid-1990s UK datasets on tenants (details not checked against the full text).

## Findings
- Benefit receipt fell by 10-15% in the treatment group relative to controls.
- Rents paid fell by 6-11%, with no evidence that this reflected lower housing consumption (smaller or worse dwellings).
- Between 60% and two-thirds of the incidence of the subsidy cut fell on landlords.
- An earlier working-paper version (CEP DP 597) found support for a "matching" (search) model of the rental market rather than perfect competition.

## Limitations
Identifies incidence of a cut, not of an increase; responses may be asymmetric. Survey data on rents; 1990s UK private rented market. Abstract-level verification only.

## Relevance
Together with Fack (2006), shows large landlord capture of housing allowances in tight markets, from a different country and the opposite direction of policy change. Relevant to the Spanish Bono Alquiler Joven and to the youth rental aid in the 2026-2030 State Housing Plan.
