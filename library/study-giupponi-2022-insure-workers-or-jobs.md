## Question
In a recession, should policy insure workers (unemployment insurance) or jobs (short-time work subsidies that keep workers attached to firms)?

## Method and data
Narrative review by researchers who produced part of the evidence (Giupponi and Landais on Italy). Combines a conceptual framework of when labour hoarding is inefficiently low (liquidity constraints, wage and hours rigidities, costly job destruction) with quasi-experimental evidence on STW and UI from the Great Recession and COVID-19.

## Findings (abstract)
- UI and STW are complements: they cover different types of workers and different kinds of shocks.
- STW works well against large, temporary shocks, where it prevents layoffs that would be costly for society.
- STW is less effective against persistent shocks, which require workers and resources to move across firms and sectors.
- Conclusion: STW should be part of the recession toolkit alongside UI, not in place of it.

## Limitations
Not a systematic review; the authors' framework shapes the synthesis (graded C rather than A). No pooled effect size.

## Relevance
The key distinction for an AI/automation shock: if it is structural and permanent for some tasks and occupations, STW (Spain's ERTE, the sectoral RED mechanism) risks keeping workers in declining jobs, and the review's logic points to worker-based insurance (UI) plus reallocation support instead. If adoption causes temporary firm-level disruption, STW is the better-suited tool.
