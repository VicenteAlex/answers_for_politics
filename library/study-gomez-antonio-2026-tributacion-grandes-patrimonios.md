## Question
What is the state of the debate on taxing high-net-worth individuals, and what do data and evidence say for Spain?

## Method and data
Narrative review of international initiatives (OECD, G20, UN, EU), comparative law of wealth taxes, descriptive statistics on Spanish wealth from AEAT administrative records (IRPF, IP, corporate tax and informative returns; 96.5% household coverage in the common-regime territory, 2017-2023), and a review of arguments.

## Findings
- Spain (AEAT records): the wealthiest 1% held 24% of wealth in 2017 and nearly 30% in 2023; the top 1% hold mostly financial assets, with unlisted corporate equity the main component.
- Family-business shares that qualify are exempt from IP and get a 95% reduction in inheritance tax (99% in Madrid, Andalusia and Murcia), which may incentivise placing personal assets in companies.
- Instruments such as SICAVs, SOCIMIs, investment and pension funds defer income taxation until realisation.
- Summarises international evidence: migration responses mostly small across borders, larger within decentralised countries; real effects on saving/investment small; major responses are reporting, avoidance and evasion.
- Notes that no Spanish study comparable to US/French administrative effective-rate studies exists; cites Fedea (López Laborda et al. 2025) for regressivity at the top 1%.
- Describes the ITSGF (Ley 38/2022) and its indefinite extension by RDL 8/2023 pending reform of regional financing.

## Limitations
Not peer reviewed; government-affiliated authors; the "arguments against" section rebuts critics and gives limited weight to evidence of large avoidance (e.g. Brülhart et al. 2022) or to administrative costs. Several figures are secondary summaries.

## Relevance
Official-leaning synthesis that states the Ministry of Finance's analytical framing; useful source of Spanish administrative wealth-concentration data and of the legal design of IP/ITSGF.
