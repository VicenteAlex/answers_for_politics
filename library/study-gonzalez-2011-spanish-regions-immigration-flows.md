## Question
How did Spanish regions absorb the 2001-2006 immigration wave, and did it affect wages or employment of low-skilled workers?

## Method and data
Instrumental variables: regional inflows are predicted from immigrant settlement patterns of the 1980s (shift-share instrument). Regional Labour Force Survey (EPA) and census data.

## Findings (abstract)
- The foreign-born share rose from 6% to 13%, with a total inflow above three million.
- Relatively unskilled immigrant inflows did not reduce wages or employment rates of unskilled workers in receiving regions.
- The extra unskilled labour was absorbed through higher total employment, via changes in skill intensity within industries rather than changes in regional output mix. Main absorbing sectors: retail, construction, hotels and restaurants, domestic services.

## Limitations
- Strong expansion period (housing boom): labour demand was rising; results may not hold in a recession (the unemployment rate of foreign nationals reached 39% in early 2013, KB indicator es-unemployment-rate-foreign).
- Shift-share instruments can be biased if the pull factors that attracted early migrants persist.
- Only 17 regions: limited variation; local or occupation-level effects may be diluted.

## Relevance
Main quasi-experimental evidence on wage/employment effects of immigration in Spain.
