## Question
How much of Spain's 1998-2008 housing boom (prices and construction) was caused by immigration?

## Method and data
Province-level panel; immigrant inflows instrumented with the historical distribution of immigrants of each origin across provinces (shift-share).

## Findings (IZA working-paper abstract)
- Average provincial inflow equal to 17% of the initial working-age population.
- This inflow raised housing prices by about 52% and was responsible for about 37% of new housing units built, roughly one third of the boom in both prices and construction.
- A 2011 presentation version covering 2000-2010 reports different magnitudes (about 25% of the price increase and 60% of the stock increase), so the published figures should be checked before precise use.

## Limitations
- Shift-share instrument assumptions; boom period with very loose credit, so the price response may be larger than in tighter credit conditions.
- Published-article figures not verified.

## Relevance
Main Spanish causal evidence that population growth through immigration raises housing demand and prices when supply is inelastic; also that it raises construction where supply can respond. Relevant to current debates linking immigration and housing affordability (and to proposals of "prioridad nacional" in social housing).
