## Question
Did the 2019 minimum wage increase in Spain raise the probability that affected workers lose their job (extensive margin) or have their working hours reduced (intensive margin)?

## Method and data
Treatment group: employees earning less than the new minimum wage before the reform. Control group: employees earning somewhat more, assumed unaffected. Outcomes: monthly transitions into unemployment and reductions in the number of working hours, tracked for one year after the reform. Secondary summaries describe the matching approach as coarsened exact matching on Spanish administrative records (MCVL); this detail was not checked against the full text.

## Findings (abstract)
- After one year, the new minimum wage significantly increased the probability of experiencing unemployment by 1.7 percentage points for treated workers.
- It increased the probability of a reduction in work intensity by 0.9 percentage points.
- Substantial heterogeneity by age, prior work intensity, economic sector and region.

## Limitations
- Same identification concern raised by Banco de España (DO 2113): workers just above the minimum may not be a valid counterfactual for those at it (different attachment to the labour market), which can bias estimates in either direction.
- Individual transitions of incumbents; effects on hiring of new entrants are not the main outcome.
- Funded by the Ministry of Labour (per Crossref), the ministry that promoted the increase; a reason for scrutiny, not for dismissal.

## Relevance
Peer-reviewed ex post estimate for Spain's largest SMI increase; smaller in magnitude than the Banco de España ex ante projection. Relevant to how wage floors interact with an AI/automation shock: adjustment came through both job loss and fewer hours.
