## Question
What did modern industrial robots contribute to productivity, prices and employment?

## Method and data
New panel of robot adoption by industry in 17 countries (IFR data), 1993-2007; instrumental variables based on robots' comparative advantage in specific tasks (e.g. reaching, handling).

## Findings (abstract)
- Increased robot use contributed approximately 0.36 percentage points to annual labour productivity growth; it also raised TFP and lowered output prices.
- No significant reduction in total employment; reduced employment share of low-skilled workers.

## Limitations
- Grade C rather than B: cross-country industry-level panel; the task-based instrument is plausible but the exclusion restriction is debated, and industry-level estimates cannot capture spillovers across industries.
- "No significant effect" on hours is not the same as a precisely estimated zero.
- Period ends 2007.

## Relevance
Spain is among the 17 countries. Supports the pattern "productivity gains plus compositional shifts against low-skilled workers" rather than mass job loss; whether this carries over to AI, which targets cognitive tasks, is an open question.
