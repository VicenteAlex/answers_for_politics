## Question
When returns to capital differ across individuals, is it better to tax wealth or capital income?

## Method and data
Calibrated OLG model with entrepreneurs who have persistent differences in productivity/returns; compares revenue-neutral replacement of the capital income tax by a wealth tax and computes optimal taxes.

## Findings
- With equal returns the two taxes are equivalent; with return heterogeneity they have opposite implications.
- A wealth tax penalises low-return holders ("use it or lose it"), reallocating capital toward high-return entrepreneurs and raising productivity.
- The optimal wealth tax is positive and yields large welfare gains (the working paper reported about 8% consumption-equivalent gains for a revenue-neutral replacement; not verified in the final version); the optimal capital income tax is negative.

## Limitations
Model-based; results depend on calibration of return heterogeneity, the absence of avoidance/evasion and on a broad base without exemptions; does not model migration.

## Relevance
Theoretical counterpoint to the OECD (2018) view that wealth taxes are more distortive than capital income taxes; relevant to Spain's debate on whether to tax wealth or to reform capital income taxation (savings base, holding companies).
