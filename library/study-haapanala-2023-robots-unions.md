## Question
Does union density moderate the effect of industrial robot exposure on industrial employment and unemployment in advanced economies?

## Method and data
Random-effects within-between regression models on a pseudo-panel of observations from 27 European countries and the United States, 1998-2019. Robot exposure is interacted with national union density.

## Findings (abstract)
- With rising robot exposure, higher union density is associated with a greater decline in industry-sector employment for younger workers and workers with lower secondary education.
- The unemployment rate declines more strongly in countries with low union density when robot exposure increases.
- Workers with tertiary education and labour market tenure are the main beneficiaries of technological change.
- Authors' interpretation: robot exposure promotes labour-market dualization in strongly unionized countries; the working-paper version describes unions as exacerbating insider-outsider effects within industry, not fully passed on to unemployment.

## Limitations
- Country-level union density is not exogenous and correlates with other institutions (employment protection, bargaining coverage, welfare). Grade C.
- Industrial robots, not software/AI; effect sizes are not given in the abstract.
- Within-between models identify associations over time within countries, not causal effects.

## Relevance
Spain has low union density but high bargaining coverage and a strongly dual (temporary/permanent) labour market. The insider-outsider pattern suggests an AI/automation shock may fall mostly on young and low-educated entrants in systems that protect incumbents; how this maps onto Spain's institutional mix is not tested directly.
