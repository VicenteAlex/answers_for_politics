## Question
What were the immediate effects of Berlin's 2020 rent freeze (first-generation-style freeze plus caps and mandated reductions) on rents and on the supply of rental housing?

## Method and data
Hedonic difference-in-differences comparing units inside and outside the policy's scope (pre-2014 buildings covered; newer buildings exempt), plus spatial regression discontinuity at Berlin's border; rental advertisements with detailed characteristics.

## Findings
- No immediate effect at announcement; advertised rents dropped significantly once the policy took effect, opening a rent gap at Berlin's border.
- Substitution: asking rents rose in adjacent unregulated municipalities such as Potsdam.
- Quantity: a sharp reduction in advertised rental units, making searches harder for newcomers, young first-time renters and movers; part of the loss may be permanent for the rental sector.
- Landlords appear to have hedged against the risk of the law being struck down (shadow rents), shifting risk to tenants.
- The authors conclude that negative consequences for renters seem to outweigh the positive ones. The abstract gives no magnitudes.

## Limitations
Short window (the Constitutional Court annulled the law in April 2021). Ads measure flows into the visible market, not the stock of contracts; fewer ads can reflect less turnover as well as withdrawal.

## Relevance
Strict freeze rather than an index-based cap; an upper bound for Spain's second-generation design. Relevant for the listings-vs-contracts measurement issue in Barcelona.
