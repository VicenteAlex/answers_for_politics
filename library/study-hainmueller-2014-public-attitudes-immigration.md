## Question
What explains individual attitudes toward immigration?

## Method and data
Narrative review of observational surveys and survey/field experiments.

## Findings
- Little support for "political economy" self-interest models (labour-market competition, fiscal burden on one's own taxes) as the main driver.
- Attitudes are shaped by sociotropic concerns about immigration's national impacts, especially cultural ones, and to a lesser extent economic ones.
- The authors call for better causal identification of how and when sociotropic concerns matter.

## Limitations
Review up to 2014; little Southern European evidence.

## Relevance
Explains why the political salience of immigration (and support for restrictive programs) need not track measured labour-market or fiscal effects; relevant to reading Spanish opinion data (CIS) and party strategies.
