## Question
Does the empirical literature find that technological change destroys more jobs than it creates?

## Method and data
Systematic review of empirical studies over four decades, classified into five technology groups (ICT, robots, innovation, TFP-style, other) and by mechanism (replacement, reinstatement, real-income effects). 127 studies in peer-reviewed journals published 1988-2021 (secondary summary; the count was not in the abstract retrieved).

## Findings (abstract)
- The labour-displacing effect of technology appears more than offset by compensating mechanisms that create or reinstate labour, for most technology types.
- Fears of widespread technological unemployment lack an empirical base.
- Blue-collar workers have been adversely affected; up- and reskilling and targeted support remain priorities.

## Limitations
- Graded B rather than A: a vote-counting systematic review without a pooled meta-analytic effect size, mixing designs of varying quality.
- Covers literature to 2021: excludes generative AI.

## Relevance
Base rate for past technologies: net employment effects small to positive, with distributional losses concentrated on specific groups. Whether generative AI follows this base rate is the open question.
