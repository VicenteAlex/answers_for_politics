## Question
Have AI chatbots changed earnings, hours and the structure of work in exposed occupations in the first two years after ChatGPT?

## Method and data
Two large adoption surveys (late 2023 and 2024) covering 11 exposed occupations (about 25,000 workers, 7,000 workplaces), linked to Danish matched employer-employee registers. Difference-in-differences by adoption and by employer encouragement policies.

## Findings (abstract)
- Rapid adoption: most employers in exposed occupations have chatbot initiatives; workers report productivity benefits; new AI-related tasks are widespread.
- Precise null effects on earnings and recorded hours at worker and workplace level, ruling out effects larger than 2% two years after ChatGPT's launch (NBER version). Earlier BFI version reported "precise zeros" with average time savings of 3% (secondary).
- Work is reorganised (content generation, AI oversight, integration); adopters move into higher-paying occupations where chatbots are more relevant, too few to move averages.

## Limitations
- Not peer-reviewed. Grade B: quasi-experimental with administrative outcomes, but adoption is not randomised.
- Short horizon (to about 2024); Denmark only; 11 occupations; earnings/hours, not hiring of new entrants.

## Relevance
Strongest early evidence against fast aggregate displacement. Denmark has high union coverage and "flexicurity", so results may not transfer directly to Spain; the young-entrant margin (see Brynjolfsson et al. 2025, "Canaries") is not its focus.
