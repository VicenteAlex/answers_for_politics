## Question
Does retraining help workers displaced by a structural (trade) shock recover earnings?

## Method and data
TAA combines retraining incentives with extended unemployment insurance for workers whose job loss is certified as trade-related. Petitions are assigned quasi-randomly to investigators who differ in approval leniency; investigator leniency instruments for TAA approval. Employer-employee matched Census data on 300,000 workers, 20 years of earnings and re-employment outcomes.

## Findings (abstract, CES-22-05)
- TAA-approved workers have about $50,000 greater cumulative earnings ten years out, driven by both higher incomes and greater labour-force participation.
- "Yet annual returns fully depreciate over the same period."
- In the most disrupted regions, approved workers are more likely to switch industries and move to labour markets with better opportunities.
- Sustained returns appear to come from training rather than from UI transfers, pointing to a role for human capital in adjustment.

## Limitations
Working paper, not peer reviewed at access. Instrument validity relies on random case assignment and on leniency affecting outcomes only through approval. TAA bundles training and UI; separating them relies on additional variation. US context with weak baseline UI.

## Relevance
Among the closest analogues for an AI/automation displacement shock: a permanent, sector-specific loss of jobs. It suggests retraining tied to income support can raise displaced workers' earnings substantially for several years and supports industry and geographic mobility, though gains fade. Spain lacks an equivalent targeted programme; its training is mostly generic and short.
