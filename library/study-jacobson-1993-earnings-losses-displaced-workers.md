## Question
How large and how persistent are the earnings losses of workers who lose stable jobs?

## Method and data
Administrative data combining workers' earnings histories with information on their employers, for Pennsylvania workers displaced between 1980 and 1986. Earnings of high-tenure workers separating from distressed firms are tracked before and after separation and compared with workers who were not displaced (design details in the full text, not re-checked here).

## Findings (abstract)
- High-tenure workers separating from distressed firms suffer long-term losses averaging 25 percent per year.
- Losses begin mounting before separation.
- They depend only slightly on age and sex, more on local labour-market conditions and the former industry, and are not limited to a few sectors.
- Losses remain large even for workers who find new jobs in similar firms.

## Limitations
One US state in a period of deep industrial restructuring (early-1980s recession); results for high-tenure workers only; displacement is not randomly assigned (the distressed-firm design mitigates but does not remove selection).

## Relevance
The benchmark for the size of displacement losses: a structural shock that destroys firm- and industry-specific human capital costs stable workers a quarter of their earnings for years. An AI/automation shock that eliminates tasks of experienced workers in an industry fits this profile. Spain's dual market concentrates job loss on temporary workers, but displaced permanent workers would face similar specific-capital losses; Spanish estimates are not in this card.
