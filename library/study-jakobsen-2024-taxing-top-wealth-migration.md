## Question
Do the wealthy emigrate in response to wealth taxes, and what are the consequences for the domestic economy?

## Method and data
Administrative data linking wealthy individuals and their firms; difference-in-differences around the Swedish abolition (2007) and Danish reforms.

## Findings
- Significant migration responses: each 1 pp increase in the top wealth tax rate reduces the stock of wealthy taxpayers by about 2%.
- Many movers own businesses that are harmed when the owner leaves.
- Aggregate effects are modest: per 1 pp, employment -0.02%, investment -0.07%, value added -0.10%.
- Per IEF (2026) summary: in Sweden, the taxable wealth of emigrants accounted for about 0.09% of total taxable wealth; revenue implications of migration were small relative to the cost of abolition (not verified in the paper).

## Limitations
Scandinavian setting with strong reporting; reforms concerned moderately high rates; external validity for billionaires with globally mobile wealth is uncertain. Card based on the abstract.

## Relevance
Best causal evidence on cross-border flight from wealth taxes; informs claims about emigration of wealthy Spaniards (e.g. to Portugal, Andorra, Italy), for which no comparable Spanish causal study was found.
