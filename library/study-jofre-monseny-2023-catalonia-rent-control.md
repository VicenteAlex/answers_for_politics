## Question
Did Catalonia's 2020 high-coverage rent control reduce rents, and did it shrink the rental market?

## Method and data
The 2020 law applied to municipalities over 20,000 inhabitants with tight markets: new contracts could not exceed a dwelling-specific reference index nor the previous rent for the unit (higher caps for recent buildings); ads and contracts had to state the cap. Register microdata of all tenancy agreements signed and ended in Catalonia 2016-2022 (rental deposit register), aggregated to municipality-quarter; difference-in-differences and event studies comparing regulated municipalities with tight-market municipalities that missed the population criterion. Robustness to Covid-19 confounding.

## Findings
- Average rents paid fell by about 4% to 6% in regulated relative to non-regulated municipalities.
- No evidence of a reduction in rental supply measured by agreements signed, agreements ended or the active stock of rental units.
- An anticipation effect: signed agreements increased just before entry into force.
- The number of tenancy agreements in neighbouring municipalities was unaffected.

## Limitations
Short window (about 18 months, ended by STC 37/2022), during the pandemic; the register counts contracts, not dwellings withdrawn to sale or other uses; no measure of quality or sale prices.

## Relevance
Main causal evidence for Barcelona's area under the 2020 law. Contrasts with Monràs and García-Montalvo (same policy, different data/design), who find a ~10% fall in new contracts concentrated above the reference price.
