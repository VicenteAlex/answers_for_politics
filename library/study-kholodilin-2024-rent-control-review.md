## Question
What does the empirical literature find about the effects of rent control on rents, supply, quality, mobility, the uncontrolled segment and households?

## Method and data
Structured review that classifies 112 empirical studies (from roughly 206 works on rent control) by outcome and direction of effect. It is a vote-count style synthesis, not a meta-analysis with pooled effect sizes; studies range from descriptive to quasi-experimental.

## Findings
- Rent controls are effective at slowing rent growth for controlled dwellings (abstract).
- They also produce a wide range of adverse effects affecting society as a whole (abstract); the body tallies effects on rental supply, housing quality and maintenance, residential mobility, rents in the uncontrolled segment, and the allocation of dwellings to households.
- The author's conclusion is that policymakers should take these side effects into account in design.

## Limitations
Vote counting gives equal weight to studies of very different quality and generation of control; older studies dominate some outcomes. The author co-authored several of the reviewed studies (e.g. on Germany). Full text not opened here; the per-outcome counts should be checked before quoting.

## Relevance
Best single entry point to the international literature. For Spain, the most relevant subset is second-generation controls (Germany 2015, Catalonia 2020, Berlin 2020), whose effects are smaller and more contested than those of strict first-generation freezes.
