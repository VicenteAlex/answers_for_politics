## Question
Which types of active labour market programmes improve participants' employment in Europe, and does the institutional or macroeconomic context matter?

## Method and data
Meta-analysis of 137 programme evaluations from 19 European countries. Regressions of whether an evaluation finds a positive and significant effect on programme type and contextual factors, including labour-market institutions and the business cycle.

## Findings (abstract)
- "It is almost exclusively the program type that seems to matter for program effectiveness"; contextual factors such as labour-market institutions and the business cycle contribute little.
- Direct employment programmes in the public sector often have detrimental effects on participants' employment.
- Wage subsidies and "services and sanctions" (job search assistance, monitoring) can increase participants' employment probability.
- Training, the most commonly used type, shows modestly positive effects.

## Limitations
Vote-count style outcome (sign and significance) rather than standardised effect sizes; evaluations of mixed quality; mostly pre-2007 programmes; short follow-up in many studies (Card, Kluve and Weber 2018 later show training gains grow over 2-3 years).

## Relevance
Spain has relied heavily on hiring subsidies and public employment schemes (e.g. local employment plans). The evidence favours job search assistance and private-sector wage subsidies over public job creation for short-run placement. For workers displaced by AI/automation, the training result should be read with Card et al. (2018): modest early, larger later.
