## Question
Which Spanish manufacturing firms adopt robots, and what happens to their output, labour share, employment and productivity afterwards?

## Method and data
27-year panel of Spanish manufacturing firms (1990-2016). Difference-in-differences with propensity-score reweighting to handle non-random adoption; structural TFP estimation in which robots enter the productivity process.

## Findings (abstract)
- Positive selection: firms with higher prior output and labour productivity adopt; conditional on size, more skill-intensive firms are less likely to adopt.
- Adopters: output gains of 20-25% within four years, labour cost share falls by 5-7 percentage points, and net job creation at a rate of 10% relative to non-adopters.
- Robots raise TFP and are complementary to exporting.
- The paper's further result that job losses occur in non-adopting firms (business stealing) is not in the abstract and was not verified here.

## Limitations
- Grade B: firm-level DiD with reweighting; selection on unobservables remains possible.
- Firm-level "relative to non-adopters" effects do not give the net effect on industry or national employment (reallocation from non-adopters).
- Manufacturing only; robots, not AI.

## Relevance
The main causal evidence on robots using Spanish data. Under Spanish labour institutions, adopting firms expanded employment while the labour share fell: the distributional channel (lower labour share) differs from the employment channel.
