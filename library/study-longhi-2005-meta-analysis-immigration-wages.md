## Question
Across studies, how much does a rise in the immigrant share change native wages?

## Method and data
Meta-analysis and meta-regression of 348 estimates of the percentage change in native wages associated with a one-percentage-point increase in the immigrant-to-native ratio, from 18 papers; tests for publication bias and study-quality moderators.

## Findings
- "A negative but small effect of immigration on wages of native groups with similar skills appears rather robust" (working-paper abstract).
- Estimates cluster around zero and differ by country and by modelling approach (spatial correlation vs. structural/national approaches).
- The pooled point estimate is not reported in the abstract; the widely cited figure (about -0.1% wage change per 1 pp rise in the immigrant share) was not verified in the full text for this card.

## Limitations
- Mostly US studies from the 1980s-1990s; predates the national skill-cell literature (Borjas 2003) and the newer natural experiments.
- Spatial-correlation studies may understate effects if natives move away (displacement), which meta-regression only partly addresses.

## Relevance
Best available pooled summary for the "small average wage effect" claim. Average effects can hide larger losses for close substitutes (low-skilled natives, earlier immigrants).
