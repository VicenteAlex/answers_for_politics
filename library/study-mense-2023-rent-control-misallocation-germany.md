## Question
When rent control covers only part of the market, what happens to rents in the uncontrolled segment and to tenant mobility?

## Method and data
Germany's 2015 rent brake capped rents of new leases at 10% above a local reference rent in designated tight markets, exempting new construction. The authors exploit staggered adoption dates across municipalities in an event-study / difference-in-differences design (with a discontinuity-in-time design in the working paper).

## Findings
- A robust positive spillover of rent control onto free-market rents (abstract).
- Reduced mobility of renters living in rent-controlled units (abstract); the SSRN version reports the mobility drop concentrated among high-income households.
- The DIW working-paper version also reports earlier demolition of old buildings (replacement by exempt new construction).
- The abstract gives no effect sizes; magnitudes need the full text.

## Limitations
Listing data (asking rents) rather than contracts. The abstract does not report magnitudes; full text not opened.

## Relevance
Directly relevant to Spain's segmented design (Ley 12/2023 caps rents in tensioned areas while new-build and some segments are treated differently; seasonal and room rentals were outside the cap until Catalonia's 2025 law). Supports the mechanism of spillovers from regulated to unregulated segments.
