## Question
How widespread is algorithmic management (software, which may include AI, that fully or partially automates tasks traditionally done by human managers: instructing, monitoring and evaluating workers), what impacts do managers perceive, and how do firms govern its use?

## Method and data
Employer survey of over 6,000 firms (mid-level managers) in France, Germany, Italy, Japan, Spain and the United States. Descriptive tabulations of adoption, perceived effects and governance measures.

## Findings (abstract)
- Algorithmic management tools are already commonly used in most countries studied.
- Managers perceive that the tools often improve the quality of their decisions and their own job satisfaction.
- Managers also report trustworthiness concerns: unclear accountability, inability to easily follow the tools' logic, and inadequate protection of workers' health.
- The authors call it urgent to examine policy gaps to ensure trustworthy use.
- Secondary summaries report adoption of 40% of firms in Japan and lower adoption in the European countries than in the US; these country figures were not checked against the full report (OECD site blocked automated access).

## Limitations
- Descriptive, manager-reported perceptions; no worker outcomes measured (grade D).
- Cross-section; no causal link from adoption to wages, intensity or health.
- Survey of firms, not workers; selection into response unknown.

## Relevance
Spain is one of the six surveyed countries, and its 2021 "Ley Rider" (Ley 12/2021) gave works councils a right to information on algorithms affecting working conditions. The survey documents that AI already reorganises management, not only tasks; evidence on effects on workers in Spain remains missing.
