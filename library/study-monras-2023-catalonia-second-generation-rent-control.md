## Question
What were the effects of Catalonia's 2020 second-generation rent cap along the whole distribution of rents relative to the unit-specific reference price?

## Method and data
Data from the Agència de l'Habitatge de Catalunya, Incasòl and the Colegio de Registradores, with the reference price computed for each unit in both treated and control municipalities. Nonparametric comparison of "excess price" histograms and difference-in-differences (also Callaway-Sant'Anna and synthetic DiD).

## Findings
- Average rents fell about 5% in treated municipalities (alternative estimators -4.1% to -4.8%); no differential pre-trends over 50+ months.
- The share of units above the reference price (about 50% before) fell by 13-22 percentage points; some non-compliance remained.
- Prices of low-price units (below reference) rose above control trends (about 3-4%), while high-price units fell by around 7-10% relative to controls.
- Overall rental supply (new contracts) fell about 10%, mostly a large drop in units above the reference price not compensated by entry below; implied elasticity of new contracts to rent of about 2.
- A quality-differentiated model rationalises this: high-quality units exit, displaced demand moves to the low-quality segment, raising its prices; welfare losses for low-quality renters.

## Limitations
Working paper, not yet peer reviewed at the time of access. Short, pandemic-era window. New contracts measure flows, not the stock. Where exiting units went (sale, own use, seasonal rental, vacancy) is not directly measured.

## Relevance
Same policy as Jofre-Monseny et al. (2023) but different conclusions on supply; the crux is data and the outcome measured (new contracts by price segment vs total agreements and active stock). Key for the claim that caps hurt renters at the bottom of the distribution.
