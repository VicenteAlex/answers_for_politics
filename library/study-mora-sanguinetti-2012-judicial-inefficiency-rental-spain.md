## Question
Does slow or costly judicial enforcement of rental contracts (evictions for non-payment) reduce rental supply and push Spain toward owner-occupation?

## Method and data
Panel data across Spanish provinces, exploiting differences in judicial efficiency (e.g. duration and congestion of civil courts) and controlling for other determinants of tenure.

## Findings
- After controls, the degree of judicial inefficiency has a "positive, although minor" effect on differences in the share of owner-occupied housing across provinces (working-paper abstract).
- Mechanism proposed: longer eviction procedures lower landlords' expected returns and limit rental supply.
- No numerical effect sizes verified (abstract only).

## Limitations
Observational cross-provincial variation; judicial efficiency may correlate with unobserved local factors. Predates the 2020-2025 eviction suspension; it concerns general court speed, not a targeted moratorium for vulnerable households. Effect described as small.

## Relevance
The only credible Spain-specific empirical study located on how eviction enforcement relates to rental supply. It supports a small negative supply channel; it does not estimate the effect of the 2020-2025 suspension (RDL 11/2020), which applied only to vulnerable households and, for occupants without title, only to dwellings of legal persons or owners of more than ten homes. No causal evaluation of that suspension, of the Bono Alquiler Joven's incidence or of the ICO guarantee line on prices was found (search of 26/11/2026).
