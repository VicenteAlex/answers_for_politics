## Question
What are the effects of immigration on US wages, employment, growth and public budgets, at all levels of government and over time?

## Method and data
Expert-committee review of the literature (peer-reviewed by NAS procedures) plus static fiscal accounting by generation and 75-year net present value projections by education of the immigrant.

## Findings (report summary page)
- Long-run impact on overall native wages and employment is "very small"; negative effects, where found, fall mostly on prior immigrants and native high-school dropouts.
- First-generation immigrants are more costly to governments (mainly state and local, through education of children) than natives; the second generation is among the strongest net fiscal contributors.
- Overall positive effect on long-run economic growth.
- (In the full report, not re-opened here: net fiscal effects are strongly increasing in the immigrant's education and depend on age at arrival.)

## Limitations
US institutions (federal/state split, smaller welfare state). Fiscal projections are model-dependent.

## Relevance
The most comprehensive consensus review; widely used as reference on both labour-market and fiscal questions. Transfer to Spain requires adjusting for a larger pay-as-you-go pension system and a lower average education of inflows.
