## Question
Does longer unemployment insurance (UI) only delay reemployment, or does it also let workers find better jobs?

## Method and data
Regression discontinuity at age 40 in Austria, the eligibility cutoff for a nine-week extension of potential UI duration on top of a 30-week base. Austrian administrative data on unemployment spells, subsequent jobs, wages and employer characteristics. A search model with duration dependence frames two opposing effects: higher reservation wages versus skill/wage losses from longer unemployment.

## Findings
IZA working-paper introduction (numbers as stated):
- The nine-week extension makes workers stay jobless two days longer.
- It causes workers to obtain jobs that pay on average 0.5 percent higher wages; the positive wage effect persists.
AER abstract (primary):
- UI raises wages mainly by improving the quality of reemployment firms (the firm component), reducing wage losses.
- Heterogeneity in matching functions can generate a negative relation between UI duration effects and wage effects, seen in their sample and across studies.

## Limitations
Small policy change (nine weeks) for workers around age 40; numbers are from the 2015 working-paper version and the published estimates may differ slightly.

## Relevance
For displaced workers in an AI/automation shock, it suggests that moderate extensions of benefit duration need not just delay reemployment: they can improve job matches. Spain's contributory benefit already has long potential duration (up to two years), so the relevant margin there differs.
