## Question
What is the current and potential impact of AI on jobs, job quality and labour policy in OECD countries?

## Method and data
Occupational risk of automation based on expert assessments of how many of 100 skills and abilities are easily automatable (more than 25 → "highest risk"); case studies; OECD surveys of workers and employers in manufacturing and finance in seven countries (carried out before ChatGPT); review of policy (skills, social dialogue, regulation).

## Findings
- Occupations at highest risk of automation, taking AI into account, account for about 27% of employment (press release wording).
- Low- and middle-skill jobs (construction, farming, fishing, forestry; to a lesser extent production and transport) are most at risk; high-skill jobs are more exposed to recent AI progress but least at risk of automation.
- Survey: three in five workers worry about losing their job to AI within ten years; two-thirds of workers already using AI say automation made their jobs less dangerous or tedious (secondary summaries).

## Limitations
- Grade D: expert-rated automatability, descriptive surveys; "risk" ≠ outcome. Figures checked against secondary reporting because the OECD site blocked direct access; Spain's country value was not verified.
- Pre-generative-AI survey.

## Relevance
The report devotes attention to social dialogue and collective bargaining in AI adoption (chapter content not verified in detail here), the channel through which Spanish labour institutions could moderate impact. Spain's own 27%-type figure was not verified; a later OECD report (Job Creation and Local Economic Development 2024) is cited by Funcas (2026) as giving Spain 27.4% exposure to generative AI vs 26% OECD average (secondary, unverified).
