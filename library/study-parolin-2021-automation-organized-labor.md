## Question
Does organised labour change how automation (proxied by occupational routine task intensity) affects occupational earnings and employment?

## Method and data
Individual-level data on 3.3 million employed US adults, 1983-2017. Interacts occupational routine task intensity (RTI) with union coverage in the worker's state and industry, and runs counterfactual simulations holding union coverage at 1983 levels.

## Findings (abstract)
- Organised labour has two countervailing effects. Higher union coverage within a state and industry inhibits the decline in earnings returns to an occupation's RTI.
- Higher union coverage hastens the decline in employment shares of high-RTI occupations.
- Counterfactual: if US union coverage had stayed at 1983 levels, the earnings returns to RTI might not have declined from 1983 to 2017 and the 1990s occupational earnings polarisation might not have occurred, but mean occupational RTI might have declined by an additional 21 percent.

## Limitations
- Union coverage is not randomly assigned; state-industry fixed effects reduce but do not remove confounding. Grade C.
- RTI is a proxy for automation exposure, not observed technology adoption.
- Counterfactuals assume coefficients are stable when coverage changes.

## Relevance
Suggests a trade-off for Spain, where collective agreements cover most employees through sectoral bargaining: stronger bargaining can protect pay in exposed occupations while accelerating the shrinking of employment in them. For an AI shock this points to insider protection alongside fewer entry jobs; consistent with Haapanala, Marx and Parolin (2023) for Europe.
