## Question
Did the 2024 application of Ley 12/2023 rent caps in Catalonia's tensioned areas reduce rent growth, and did it affect the number of tenancy agreements?

## Method and data
Public Generalitat data on average rents and tenancy agreements by municipality and quarter. Treated: 140 municipalities declared in March 2024 (the 131 second-wave municipalities are excluded). Control: up to 680 municipalities, but rent data exist for only 173 and the matched control group is small (effective size 24). Two-way fixed-effects DiD, event studies, propensity-score-matched DiD and synthetic DiD.

## Findings
- Preferred DiD: rent growth -0.030 (SE 0.011), about 3 percentage points lower (about 20.5 euros per month); tenancy agreements -6.6 per 10,000 inhabitants (SE 1.1), about 13% below a pre-treatment mean of 42.
- PSM-DiD: rent effect +0.007 (not significant); agreements -6.3 (significant). Synthetic DiD: rent effect -0.002 (not significant); agreements about -5.
- Joint tests reject parallel pre-trends for rent growth (p = 0.022) and agreements (p < 0.001).
- Author's reading: a reduction in tenancy agreements (about 5-7 per 10,000 inhabitants) is robust; the effect on rent growth is not.

## Limitations
Preprint; pre-trend violations; small and possibly unrepresentative controls; aggregated data; short post-period (2024). Fewer new agreements can reflect lower turnover (tenants staying) as well as supply withdrawal; the paper cannot separate them.

## Relevance
Only causal-design study found on the 2024 zonas tensionadas regime. Evidence is weak but points to fewer new contracts; it does not measure sale prices or conversions to sale.
