## Question
Using a data-driven control group, did the Mariel inflow lower wages or employment of low-skilled workers in Miami?

## Method and data
Synthetic control method: a weighted combination of US cities that best reproduces Miami's pre-1980 labour-market trends. Outcome: wages and unemployment of non-Cuban high-school dropouts.

## Findings
- "No significant difference in the wages of workers in Miami relative to its control after 1980" (abstract).
- Matching on a short pre-1979 series and using small subsamples, as Borjas (2017) does, can generate large spurious wage differences attributed to measurement error.

## Limitations
- Same underlying small CPS samples; confidence intervals cannot rule out moderate effects.
- Effect sizes not retrieved for this card (paywalled full text).

## Relevance
Together with Card (1990) and Borjas (2017) it shows that conclusions from one episode hinge on sample and control choices; meta-analytic and multi-episode evidence is needed (see study-longhi-2005-meta-analysis-immigration-wages).
