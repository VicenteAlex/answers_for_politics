## Question
What happened to jobs and wages when Portugal reduced the maximum standard workweek from 44 to 40 hours in 1996?

## Method and data
Difference-in-differences comparing workers whose hours were above 40 before the reform (directly affected) with workers already at or below 40 hours, using Portuguese worker-level data. Outcomes: job separation, hours, hourly and monthly wages.

## Findings (abstract)
- For workers involved, the reduction reduced the job separation rate and increased hourly wages.
- Monthly earnings remained approximately constant (hourly wages rose to compensate for fewer hours).
- Workers working less than 40 hours per week were also affected and were more likely to lose their job.
- The 2008 IZA working-paper version reported that the reduction did not increase job loss of directly affected workers; the published version's results are those cited here.

## Limitations
- Short format (Economics Letters); effects on hiring and aggregate employment are not reported in the abstract.
- The control group (workers below 40 hours) was itself affected, which complicates interpretation of the DiD.
- Data source and design details were not checked against the published full text.

## Relevance
Portugal's reform is the closest Southern European precedent to Spain's 2025 proposal to cut the statutory workweek to 37.5 hours without pay cuts. It shows lower separations for directly affected incumbents but adverse effects for others, and no evidence of work-sharing job creation. For an AI shock, it suggests hours cuts mainly reprice labour per hour rather than spreading employment.
