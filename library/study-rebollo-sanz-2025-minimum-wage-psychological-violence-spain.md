## Question
Did Spain's 22% minimum wage increase in 2019 affect intimate partner violence?

## Method and data
Nationally representative Survey of Violence Against Women. Treated women: high predicted probability of holding minimum-wage jobs. Doubly robust difference-in-differences with inverse probability weighting.

## Findings (abstract)
- No effect on physical or sexual intimate partner violence.
- 42% increase in psychological violence among treated women.
- Mechanism: the reform shifted employment from women to their partners, reducing women's bargaining power; for women with partners five or more years older, other mechanisms (gender-role disruption, instrumental violence) are suggested.

## Limitations
- Working paper, not yet peer reviewed; graded B for design but should be read as provisional.
- Survey-reported violence; treatment assigned by predicted, not actual, minimum-wage status.
- Outcome is intimate partner violence, not street crime.

## Relevance
One of very few causal studies linking a Spanish national policy of 2015-2026 to a crime-related outcome. It does not address street or property crime.
