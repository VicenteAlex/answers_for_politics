## Question
Does a wealth tax reduce household saving?

## Method and data
Norway's 2010 model for assessing housing wealth used geographic fixed effects, producing discontinuities in assessed (taxable) housing wealth at area borders even without discontinuities in market prices. Combined with third-party-reported financial data, this isolates variation in wealth-tax liabilities.

## Findings
- Higher wealth-tax exposure causes households to save more: each additional NOK of wealth tax raises annual net financial saving by 3.76 NOK, enough to offset current and future wealth taxes.
- The extra saving is financed mainly by higher labor earnings.
- Small negative effects of higher marginal rates are outweighed by larger positive effects of higher average rates; income effects appear to dominate substitution effects.

## Limitations
Identifies effects for the broad base of Norwegian wealth-tax payers (low threshold), not necessarily for billionaires or business owners; housing-assessment variation affects average more than marginal rates.

## Relevance
Contradicts the common claim that wealth taxes necessarily reduce saving; relevant to Spain where most IP payers are upper-middle-wealth households, less so to the ITSGF's very top.
