## Question
How could a progressive wealth tax be implemented in the US, what would it raise and how would it affect wealth concentration?

## Method and data
Estimates of top wealth from distributional national accounts and rich lists; discussion of enforcement (third-party reporting, valuation, exit tax); simulation of the Forbes-400 wealth share under counterfactual wealth taxes.

## Findings
- Tax base for the top 0.1%: about USD 9-13 trillion depending on wealth measurement.
- A 3% marginal rate above USD 1bn since 1982 would have reduced the top-400 wealth share in 2018 from about 3.5% to about 2%; a 10% rate, to about 1%.

## Limitations
Simulations assume behavioural responses limited mainly to the design described (strong enforcement, no exemptions); critics (e.g. Scheuer and Slemrod 2021) note that European experience shows larger avoidance where bases are narrow. BPEA conference volume (discussant-reviewed, not standard peer review). Authors are advocates of the tax.

## Relevance
Foundational reference for the "progressive wealth tax to curb concentration" argument used by Spanish proposals (Sumar, Podemos).
