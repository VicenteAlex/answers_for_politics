## Question
What determines how responsive housing supply is to prices across cities?

## Method and data
Satellite-generated measures of land unavailable for development (slopes above 15%, water bodies) within 50 km of US metro centres; supply elasticities estimated with demand-shifter instruments; regulation (Wharton index) treated as endogenous.

## Findings
- Steep terrain effectively curtails residential development; most metro areas with inelastic supply are severely land-constrained by geography (abstract).
- Supply elasticities depend on both physical and regulatory constraints, and regulation is itself influenced by prices and demographic growth (abstract).
- The paper's metro-level elasticity estimates are widely used; they are not reproduced here (full text not opened).

## Limitations
US context; elasticities are long-run metro averages.

## Relevance
Foundational evidence that the price response to demand depends on supply elasticity. Barcelona (sea, Collserola hills, built-out municipality) is a geographically constrained market, which implies that demand shocks (population, tourism, income) translate largely into prices for both rent and sale, independently of rent regulation.
