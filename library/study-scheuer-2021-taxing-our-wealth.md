## Question
What can international experience and optimal-tax theory tell us about annual taxes on household wealth?

## Method and data
Narrative review of empirical studies on European wealth taxes, related taxes (property, estate) and optimal tax theory.

## Findings
- Of a dozen OECD countries that once had annual wealth taxes only three kept them (at time of writing); only Switzerland collects revenue (as share of total) comparable to US proposals.
- Empirical studies find that wealth taxes sometimes, but not always, cause large responses in saving, portfolios, avoidance and evasion; outcomes depend on design (base breadth, exemptions) and enforcement.
- Because US proposals differ greatly from past European taxes (very high thresholds, broad base, strong enforcement), only general lessons transfer.

## Limitations
Narrative review; predates several key studies (Ring 2025; Jakobsen et al. 2024; Londoño-Vélez and Ávila-Mahecha 2025). Card based on abstract.

## Relevance
Balanced synthesis often cited by both critics and supporters; frames the key cruxes for Spain: exemptions (business assets), valuation and enforcement.
