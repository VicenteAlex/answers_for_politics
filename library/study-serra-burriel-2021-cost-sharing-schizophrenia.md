## Question
Did the 2012 introduction of co-payment for pensioners reduce adherence and increase hospitalisations among people with schizophrenia?

## Method and data
DiD with patient fixed effects, leads and lags. Exposed: pensioners (0%→10%, capped); controls: non-pensioners with 0% (exempt groups). Integrated Valencian health databases, 1 year before/after.

## Findings
- Adherence OR 0.70 (99% CI 0.66-0.75); antipsychotic exposure OR 0.85 (0.83-0.88); prescription OR 0.91 (0.88-0.93).
- All-cause hospitalisation OR 1.13 (1.05-1.23); schizophrenia hospitalisation OR 1.18 (1.08-1.29).
- No pre-reform differences; effects lasted about one year.

## Limitations
One region, one disease, short window; no full cost-effectiveness analysis.

## Relevance
Only study found linking the 2012 co-payment to hospitalisations: small caps can still matter for vulnerable chronic patients.
