## Question
Can a Member State make access to a social-assistance benefit conditional on ten years of residence when the claimant is a long-term resident third-country national?

## Method and data
Preliminary reference from the Tribunale di Napoli; legal interpretation of Directive 2003/109/EC (long-term residents), Art. 11(1)(d) (equal treatment in social security, social assistance and social protection).

## Findings
- The ten-year residence requirement (last two years continuous) for the reddito di cittadinanza is indirect discrimination against long-term residents, who acquire that status after five years (Art. 4 of the Directive); Member States cannot add a longer residence requirement for equal treatment.
- A criminal sanction for false declarations about that requirement is incompatible with the Directive where the underlying obligation itself is not.
- Related case law (secondary reporting, not read in full): C-571/10 Kamberaj (2012) on a housing benefit in Bolzano reserved by budget quota by nationality, where the Court held that Art. 11 covers housing benefits that are "core" to integration, leaving the classification to the national judge; C-747/22 (INPS, 2024) applied similar reasoning to beneficiaries of subsidiary protection.
- European Commission (Commissioner Brunner, written answer of 10 September, reported by Demócrata/Europa Press): a "national priority" clause in housing and benefits that intrinsically affects nationals of other Member States or third countries, such as a residence requirement, is indirectly discriminatory unless justified and proportionate; the Commission had not assessed the Aragón rules because they were not yet adopted.

## Limitations
- Binds only for long-term residents (five years of legal residence) and, by other directives, single-permit workers and protection beneficiaries; it does not protect irregular residents, and Art. 11(4) of the Directive allows limiting social assistance to "core benefits".
- Differences based on nationality vis-à-vis EU citizens are governed separately (Art. 18 TFEU, Directive 2004/38/EC), not analysed here.
- Spanish constitutional limits (Art. 13 and 14 CE, Constitutional Court doctrine on foreigners' rights) are not covered by this card.

## Relevance
Directly limits Vox's 2026 proposals of preferential access "de los españoles" to public housing and benefits and of IMV only for Spaniards, and the 10-year padrón requirement in the PP-Vox Extremadura agreement (es-ex-event-2026-04-16-acuerdo-pp-vox-prioridad-nacional) and the Valencian accompanying law. Requirements based on legal residence (excluding irregular residents from non-urgent benefits, as PP and Vox propose) are not covered by this ruling.
