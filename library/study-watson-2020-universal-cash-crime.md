## Question
How does a universal, unconditional cash transfer affect crime?

## Method and data
Sharp timing of the Alaska Permanent Fund Dividend, paid to virtually every resident on a known date; daily incident data, comparing days around the payment and exploiting variation in the dividend amount across years.

## Findings (abstract)
- Substance-abuse incidents rise 14% the day after payment and remain elevated for about four weeks.
- Property crime declines somewhat; no change in violent crime.
- Annual effects on crime are small and their estimated cost is a very small share of the payment.

## Limitations
- Captures the short-run effect of a lump-sum, once-a-year payment, not a monthly means-tested income floor.
- Universal transfer: most recipients are not poor.

## Relevance
Suggests that unconditional cash has small net effects on crime, with a decrease in property crime and a short-lived increase in substance-related incidents. Indirect evidence for Spain's Ingreso Mínimo Vital, which is monthly and means-tested.
