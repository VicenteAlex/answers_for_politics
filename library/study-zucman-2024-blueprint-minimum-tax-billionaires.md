## Question
How could countries coordinate to ensure that ultra-high-net-worth individuals pay a minimum effective tax?

## Method and data
Combines rich-list wealth data with estimates of taxes currently paid by billionaires; static revenue simulation with assumptions about avoidance; policy design discussion.

## Findings
- Billionaires' effective personal tax rate is about 0.3% of wealth; average real pre-tax return on ultra-high wealth about 7.5% a year over four decades.
- A minimum tax of 2% of wealth on about 3,000 billionaires would raise USD 200-250bn per year; extending to centimillionaires adds USD 100-140bn.
- Countries could implement the standard through a presumptive income tax, a broad-based income tax or a wealth tax; strengthened exit taxes and "tax collector of last resort" mechanisms would limit avoidance without universal adoption.

## Limitations
Static estimates; behavioural responses (avoidance, valuation of private firms, migration to non-participating jurisdictions) are handled by assumption. Not peer reviewed. The author is a leading advocate of the policy. Per IEF (2026), France, Spain, South Africa, Colombia and the African Union supported Brazil's proposal while the United States and Germany expressed reservations; the G20 Rio Leaders' Declaration (Nov 2024, para. 20) committed to cooperate so that ultra-high-net-worth individuals are effectively taxed, without adopting the 2% standard.

## Relevance
Basis of "Zucman-style" proposals in Spain (e.g. Sumar's 2026 bill for a 3% levy above EUR 50M) and of the Spain-Brazil initiative at FFD4 Seville (2025).
